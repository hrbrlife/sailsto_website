## Sails.to Platform Architecture — Comprehensive Summary

Based on all information provided, verified against source materials.

---

### 1. LEGAL STRUCTURE OPTIONS

**Option A: Series within Existing DAO LLC**
A convenience path for smaller issuances. The issuer launches as a dedicated Series within Sails.to's master DAO LLC structure. Faster setup, lower administrative overhead. The Series is legally isolated from other Series in the same master LLC — liability firewalls apply.

**Option B: Issuer's Own DAO LLC**
For issuers who want full independence. Sails.to facilitates formation of a new DAO LLC — either Wyoming or Marshall Islands, issuer's choice. This entity belongs to the issuer. They can take it with them. It operates under Sails.to's standardized operational framework but is legally theirs.

**In both cases:** The DAO LLC is run in a standardized way designed to fulfill the utilities of the possible smart tokens the platform supports. This standardization is what makes the ecosystem interoperable — the same apps, the same credential logic, the same settlement mechanics work regardless of which DAO LLC structure an issuer chose.

---

### 2. THE CREDENTIAL (NFT LICENSE) HIERARCHY

The platform operates via non-transferable, revocable, renewable NFT credentials issued by the Foundation that runs the network. Each participant role requires the appropriate license to operate. These are soulbound tokens — they cannot be sold or transferred, only issued and revoked by the appropriate authority.

**Licenseable Roles:**

**Issuer (NFT Licensed)**
Authorizes an entity to issue securities on the platform. Contains allowed exemptions (Reg D, Reg S, EU equivalents), permitted investor categories, jurisdiction constraints, and corporate action authority. Without this credential, no token series can be created.

**Trustee (NFT Licensed)**
A licensed trust provider appointed by the issuer as neutral overseer. Their role: audit, not touch. They have visibility into the structure, they verify compliance with the offering terms, but they do not control or custody assets. This is oversight, not management.

**Broker (NFT Licensed)**
Authorizes a broker to operate on the OTC network. Contains jurisdiction(s) of operation, permitted activities (execution, custody, KYC issuance), regulatory license references, and renewal/expiry dates. Brokers can:
- Issue KYC credentials to their clients
- Execute trades as principal (counterparty of record)
- Trade with other brokers on the OTC network
- Provide custody services
- Source liquidity for their clients

**The OTC network connection is optional.** An issuer can operate without broker involvement if they're doing direct placement to their own investor network.

**Custodian / Quasi-Paying Agent (NFT Licensed)**
Handles the bridge functionality — specifically the ISIN↔Solana conversion and the Solana↔TON wrapping. Also handles custodial functions for the TradFi bridge and can act as paying agent for distributions to investors holding via the traditional rail.

**KYC Provider (NFT Licensed from Foundation)**
A third-party entity licensed to perform KYC/AML verification on behalf of the network. This enables an investor to get certified once and then buy from multiple issuers directly — without going through KYC separately with each issuer. The KYC credential they receive is recognized across the platform (subject to issuer-specific acceptance rules).

This is distinct from broker-issued KYC. An investor can:
- Get KYC'd by their broker (credential specific to that broker relationship)
- Get KYC'd by a licensed third-party provider (credential portable across issuers)
- Get KYC'd directly by an issuer (for direct placement)

Different issuers can specify which KYC credential issuers they accept.

**PSP/MSB — Payment Service Provider / Money Services Business (Licenseable)**
A partner category for payment processing. Sails.to will provide one for their DAO structures. This enables use cases like securitization of incoming payment streams — a receivables-backed security could be structured where the underlying payments flow through the PSP, with the smart contract logic handling waterfall distributions automatically.

### 3. DISTRIBUTION MECHANICS

**Direct Placement (Issuer as Counterparty)**
The issuer places securities directly to investors in their network. No broker intermediation required. The issuer performs (or accepts third-party) KYC, the issuer is the counterparty to the transaction. Fee: 0.5% of capital raised.

**Broker-Mediated Placement (Broker as Counterparty)**
The issuer posts their offering. Licensed brokers on the OTC network can source investors from their client base. The broker — not the issuer — is counterparty of record. The broker handles KYC, suitability, execution. Fee: 6% of capital raised (this covers broker compensation).

**Secondary Trading**
Once securities are issued, investors can trade via the OTC network. Trades occur between investors and their brokers, or between brokers. The broker hop ensures compliance gates remain enforced — every transfer goes through a licensed intermediary who verifies eligibility. Fee: 0.5% per trade.

**The "Broker Hop" in Practice:**
- Investor A (client of Broker X) wants to sell
- Investor B (client of Broker Y) wants to buy
- On-chain: Investor A → Broker X → Broker Y → Investor B
- Brokers verify eligibility at each step
- Settlement is quasi-atomic (effectively atomic from user perspective, but the broker intermediation is there)

### 4. SETTLEMENT RAILS

**Primary: Solana**
Security tokens are Solana-native. Transfer restrictions are enforced on-chain via the credential system — a token can only be held by a wallet linked to an eligible identity. Settlement is quasi-atomic: when a trade executes, the cap table updates immediately. No T+2, no settlement risk in the traditional sense.

**Secondary: TON (Automatic)**
Tokens can be wrapped for the TON blockchain automatically. Same mechanics as the ISIN wrapping but without the manual setup step. This extends reach to the TON ecosystem while maintaining the same credential/compliance logic.

**Tertiary: ISIN / Clearstream (Opt-In)**
For issuers who want access to traditional finance distribution:

1. Lock tokens into a TradFi Bridge Series within the DAO LLC
2. Mint ISIN-bearing instruments against those locked tokens
3. These instruments clear through Clearstream — accessible to any private bank globally

**Cost:** ~€3-5k setup
**Timeline:** ~1 week

**Beyond ISIN: Vienna MTF Listing (Separate Step)**
If an issuer wants a publicly listed instrument (not just OTC-clearable), they can register on Vienna MTF. This is an additional step beyond ISIN registration. Same underlying economics, but now the instrument appears on a regulated exchange.

**The Result:**
An investor in Singapore can hold a security in a Solana wallet. An investor in Geneva can hold the same security — same economics, same cap table entry — as a bond in their private bank account settling through Clearstream. The issuer sees both on one dashboard. Cross-conversion is possible: an investor can move from one rail to another subject to the applicable compliance rules.

### 5. TECHNOLOGY INFRASTRUCTURE

**Melusina OS**
The operating environment. Derived from Sandstorm (the open-source self-hosted web app platform), modified for blockchain integration. Key characteristics:

- **Solana login**: Authentication via Solana wallet, not username/password
- **Per-issuance containers ("pearls")**: Each issuance gets isolated compute/storage
- **App store model**: Standardized applications shared across a central app store provided by the Foundation
- **Self-hosted**: Each financial institution can run their own Melusina server

**Multi-Server Architecture**
Financial institutions running Melusina servers are not siloed. The architecture is:

- Multiple servers, each potentially run by different institutions
- Chain is the authority (Solana is the source of truth)
- Common protocol makes all servers compatible
- An institution can run their own server OR be accumulated on shared infrastructure

This means: a broker in London, an issuer in Singapore, and a custodian in Zurich can each run their own Melusina instance — or share infrastructure — and the system remains coherent because the blockchain is the coordination layer.

**The Foundation**
Runs the network. Operates the app store. Issues the NFT licenses to participants. Sets the protocol standards. Does not custody securities or hold investor funds.

### 6. INVESTOR ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility is determined by **the laws of the investor's residency AND/OR the jurisdiction of their servicing broker**.

This is not a single global rule. It's exemption-specific and jurisdiction-specific:
- A US investor must typically be accredited (Reg D)
- A non-US investor falls under Reg S (or local equivalents)
- EU investors may fall under EU prospectus exemptions
- The servicing broker's jurisdiction adds another layer (a broker licensed in X may only serve investors eligible under X's rules)

The KYC credential encodes investor classification (accredited, professional, qualified purchaser, etc.) and jurisdiction, but the jurisdiction data is anonymized — no PII on-chain. The credential is a trust assertion: "this licensed entity verified this identity meets these criteria."

### 7. SERIES STRUCTURE WITHIN A DAO LLC

Each DAO LLC can contain multiple Series — legally isolated compartments. Typical structure:

**Operating Series**
Holds the specific assets/pledges for a given issuance. Receives revenue, holds underlying collateral.

**Treasury Series**
Operational funds, reserves, unclaimed distributions. Under trustee oversight.

**TradFi Bridge Series**
Where tokens are locked to mint ISIN-bearing instruments. The custodian/quasi-paying agent operates here.

**Additional Series as needed**
The structure is flexible. An issuer doing multiple offerings can have separate Operating Series for each. A complex securitization might have waterfall-specific Series.

Each Series is legally firewalled from the others. Creditors of one Series cannot reach assets in another.

### 8. USE CASES ENABLED

**Standard Equity/Debt Issuance**
Company raises capital by issuing tokenized securities. Direct placement or broker-distributed. Secondary trading via OTC network. Optional TradFi bridge for institutional distribution.

**Securitization of Payment Streams**
With the PSP/MSB partnership: incoming payments (receivables, royalties, subscription revenue) flow through the payment provider. Smart contract logic handles waterfall distributions to security holders. The DAO LLC structure provides the legal wrapper; the token represents the economic interest.

**Fund Structures**
A fund manager issues tokenized fund interests. Investors can hold via wallet or via their private bank. Distributions flow automatically. The fund manager sees the full cap table regardless of which rail each investor uses.
