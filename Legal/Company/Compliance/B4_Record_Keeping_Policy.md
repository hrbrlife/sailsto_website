## SECTION 1: PURPOSE

1.1. This Policy establishes record-keeping procedures to maintain liability separation between [TERM:Series] as required by Montana law, comply with money services business and money transmitter record-keeping requirements, support financial reporting and tax compliance, enable regulatory examinations, and protect the [TERM:Company] in legal matters.

## SECTION 4: RECORD FORMAT AND STORAGE

### 4.1. Acceptable Formats

4.1.1. Records may be maintained in paper format as originals or copies.

4.1.2. Records may be maintained in electronic format including PDF, spreadsheet, or database.

4.1.3. Records may be maintained in cloud storage with appropriate security.

4.1.4. Records may be maintained on blockchain for immutable transaction records.

### 4.2. Electronic Records

4.2.1. Electronic records must be accurately reproduced.

4.2.2. Electronic records must be indexed and searchable.

4.2.3. Electronic records must be protected from alteration.

4.2.4. Electronic records must have backup copies.

4.2.5. Electronic records must be readily accessible.

### 4.3. Storage Security

4.3.1. All records must be protected from unauthorized access.

4.3.2. All records must be protected from fire, flood, and disaster.

4.3.3. All records must be backed up regularly, at least weekly.

4.3.4. All records must be stored in at least two geographic locations.

### 4.4. Access Controls

4.4.1. Only authorized personnel may access records.

4.4.2. Access shall be logged.

4.4.3. Sensitive records, including KYC and financial records, require enhanced controls.

4.4.4. The [TERM:CTO] is responsible for IT security of records.
[DOC:B6]

4.4.5. Travel rule data and sanctions screening records shall be protected from alteration and retained with the same controls as AML records.

## SECTION 5: RETENTION PERIODS

### 5.1. Minimum Retention

5.1.1. Formation documents, including Articles and Operating Agreement, shall be retained permanently.

5.1.2. Series Designations shall be retained permanently.

5.1.3. Member and ownership records shall be retained permanently.

5.1.4. Financial statements shall be retained for seven years.

5.1.5. Tax returns and support shall be retained for seven years.

5.1.6. BSA/AML records shall be retained for five years per 31 CFR §1010.410(e).

5.1.7. KYC/KYB files shall be retained for five years after the relationship ends per 31 CFR §1010.410(e).

5.1.8. Transaction records shall be retained for five years per 31 CFR §1010.410(e) and §1010.430.

5.1.9. Contracts shall be retained for seven years after expiration.

5.1.10. Employment records shall be retained for seven years after termination.

5.1.11. Regulatory filings shall be retained for seven years.

5.1.12. Email and correspondence shall be retained for three years generally and five years for compliance-related correspondence.

### 5.2. Regulatory Requirements

5.2.1. If a regulatory requirement mandates longer retention, the longer period applies.

### 5.3. Litigation Hold

5.3.1. If litigation is pending or anticipated, all relevant records must be preserved regardless of retention period.

## SECTION 6: RECORD DESTRUCTION
[FLOW:Record_Destruction]

### 6.1. Destruction Authorization

6.1.1. Records may be destroyed only after the retention period expires.

6.1.2. Records may be destroyed only with written authorization from the [TERM:CFO].

6.1.3. Records may be destroyed only using secure destruction methods.

6.1.4. Records may be destroyed only with documentation of what was destroyed.

### 6.2. Secure Destruction

6.2.1. Paper records shall be destroyed by cross-cut shredding.

6.2.2. Electronic records shall be destroyed by secure deletion with verification.

6.2.3. Hardware shall be destroyed by physical destruction or certified wiping.

### 6.3. Destruction Log

6.3.1. The [TERM:CFO] shall maintain a destruction log showing description of records destroyed, date of destruction, method of destruction, and person authorizing destruction.

## SECTION 7: SERIES SEPARATION
[FLOW:Series_Separation]

### 7.1. Critical Requirement

7.1.1. Maintaining separate records for each [TERM:Series] is critical to preserving liability separation under Montana law.

### 7.2. Separation Methods

7.2.1. **Separate bank accounts for each [TERM:Series] shall be maintained.** Per MCA §35-8-304, maintaining separate assets is required to preserve the liability shield between series. Each [TERM:Series] shall have at least one dedicated bank account. Pooling of [TERM:Series] funds in a single account is prohibited except for temporary operational necessity not exceeding five business days, with documented allocation records maintained.

7.2.2. Separate sub-ledgers in the accounting system shall be maintained for each [TERM:Series].

7.2.3. A [TERM:Series] identifier shall be included on all documents.

7.2.4. Separate folders and directories shall be maintained for each [TERM:Series].

7.2.5. Clear ownership designation shall be included on all assets.

### 7.3. Asset Custody Records

7.3.1. When one [TERM:Series] holds assets for another, such as [TERM:Series_T2] holding crypto for a [TERM:ClientSeries], the custodial arrangement shall be documented in writing.

7.3.2. Records shall be maintained showing beneficial ownership.

7.3.3. Assets shall not be commingled.

7.3.4. Regular statements shall be provided to the owning [TERM:Series].

### 7.4. Monthly Reconciliation

7.4.1. The [TERM:CFO] shall ensure monthly reconciliation of each [TERM:Series]' assets and liabilities, inter-series balances, and custodial holdings.

### 7.5. Series Identification in External Dealings

7.5.1. Each [TERM:Series] shall conduct business in its own name and clearly indicate its [TERM:Series] status on all contracts, invoices, and external communications. No [TERM:Series] shall represent itself simply as "CCASH" or the [TERM:Company] generally when incurring obligations.

7.5.2. When a [TERM:Series] executes a contract, the signature block shall identify the full [TERM:Series] name, such as "[TERM:Company] CCASH Money Services (US) Series LLC – [Series Name] (Series [Designation])".

7.5.3. For material or long-term contracts, the [TERM:Company] shall seek to include a counterparty acknowledgment substantially similar to: "Counterparty acknowledges it is contracting with [Series Name], a series of [TERM:Company] CCASH Money Services (US) Series LLC, and acknowledges the limited liability structure under Montana Code Annotated Section 35-8-304. Counterparty agrees that its remedies are limited to the assets of [Series Name] and it has no recourse to other series or the [TERM:Company] generally."

7.5.4. Letterhead, invoices, and business communications for each [TERM:Series] shall clearly identify the [TERM:Series] designation.

### 7.6. Annual Separateness Audit

7.6.1. The [TERM:CFO] shall conduct an annual separateness audit verifying: (a) maintenance of separate bank accounts for each [TERM:Series]; (b) proper [TERM:Series] identification on all material contracts; (c) accurate [TERM:Series] designations in accounting records and ledgers; (d) absence of asset or liability commingling between [TERM:Series]; and (e) proper documentation of inter-series transactions and custodial arrangements.

7.6.2. The separateness audit shall be documented in a written report to the [TERM:Manager], identifying any deficiencies and corrective actions taken or planned.

7.6.3. If any commingling, improper identification, or separateness failure is discovered, immediate corrective action shall be taken, including reallocation of misdirected funds, amendment of improperly titled contracts, and restatement of accounting entries as necessary.

### 7.7. Consequences of Separateness Failures

7.7.1. Failure to maintain separateness as required by this Section may be deemed a material breach of this Policy by the responsible [TERM:Officers], subject to disciplinary action including removal.

7.7.2. [TERM:Officers] and employees shall report any known or suspected separateness failures to the [TERM:CFO] immediately upon discovery.

## SECTION 9: CROSS-BORDER DATA TRANSFERS AND DATA PROTECTION
[DOC:B8] <!-- Privacy and Data Protection Policy -->

### 9.1. Lawful Basis for Processing

9.1.1. Compliance records are processed under applicable legal obligations and legitimate interests in preventing financial crime and maintaining banking relationships.

9.1.2. Consent is obtained for processing that exceeds legal requirements, including marketing communications.

### 9.2. Cross-Border Transfers

9.2.1. Personal data may be transferred across jurisdictions where required for regulatory compliance, banking partner requirements, or law enforcement cooperation.

9.2.2. Cross-border transfers rely on appropriate legal mechanisms as required by applicable data protection laws.

9.2.3. The [TERM:CCO] maintains a record of transfer mechanisms used for each category of cross-border transfer.

### 9.3. Data Subject Rights

9.3.1. The [TERM:Company] honors data subject rights as required by applicable data protection laws, including access, rectification, and erasure where not prohibited by retention requirements.

9.3.2. **Response Timelines.** The [TERM:Company] responds to data subject requests within the timelines required by applicable law:

| **Jurisdiction** | **Initial Response** | **Extension** | **Extension Notice** |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| EEA/UK/Switzerland | 30 calendar days | Up to 60 additional days | Required prior to extension |
| California | 45 calendar days | Up to 45 additional days | Required prior to extension |
| Virginia/Colorado/Connecticut/Utah | 45 calendar days | Up to 45 additional days | Required prior to extension |
| Other U.S. States | 30 calendar days | As permitted by applicable law | Required where mandated |
| All Other Jurisdictions | 30 calendar days or as required by applicable law, whichever is shorter | As permitted by applicable law | Required where mandated |

9.3.2.1. Extensions are permitted only for complex or voluminous requests and require written notification to the requestor prior to expiration of the initial response period.

9.3.3. Compliance records cannot be erased during mandatory retention periods required by law.

### 9.4. Data Protection Assessments

9.4.1. Data protection assessments are conducted for new processing activities involving sensitive personal data as required by applicable law.

### 9.5. Data Collection Disclosures (Transparency Requirements)

<!-- schema:term TERM:DataCollectionDisclosure -->

9.5.1. **Categories of Personal Data Collected.** The [TERM:Company] collects and processes the following categories of personal data in connection with its record-keeping obligations:
   (a) **Identifiers:** Full legal name, date of birth, government identification numbers (SSN/ITIN/EIN), passport/ID document images, physical address, email address, telephone number;
   (b) **Financial Information:** Bank account details, payment card tokens (last-four only), transaction history, wallet addresses, source of funds documentation;
   (c) **KYC/KYB Records:** Identity verification results, beneficial ownership information, corporate formation documents, EDD documentation;
   (d) **Technical Data:** IP addresses, device identifiers, access logs, authentication timestamps;
   (e) **Communications:** Customer support correspondence, compliance-related communications.

9.5.2. **Purposes of Processing.** Personal data is processed for:
   (a) Customer identification and verification (CIP/KYC/KYB) per BSA requirements;
   (b) Transaction monitoring and suspicious activity detection per 31 CFR §1022.320;
   (c) Sanctions and PEP screening per OFAC requirements;
   (d) Regulatory reporting (SARs, CTRs, Form 8300);
   (e) Service delivery and account management;
   (f) Legal compliance and responding to lawful requests.

9.5.3. **Recipients of Personal Data.** Personal data may be shared with:
   (a) Banking partners and payment processors as required for service delivery;
   (b) Regulatory authorities (FinCEN, state regulators, OFAC) as required by law;
   (c) Law enforcement pursuant to valid legal process;
   (d) Third-party service providers (identity verification, sanctions screening) under DPA agreements per [DOC:B6] §8.2A;
   (e) Partner MSBs per [DOC:N1] for interstate transaction routing.

9.5.4. **Retention Periods.** See §5.1 for specific retention periods by record type. Primary retention periods:
   (a) BSA/AML records: 5 years per 31 CFR §1010.410(e);
   (b) Financial records: 7 years;
   (c) Formation documents: Permanent.

9.5.5. **Data Subject Rights.** See §9.3 and [DOC:B8] for rights exercise procedures.

9.5.6. **Notice Integration.** This Section satisfies transparency requirements under applicable data protection laws requiring disclosure of data collection practices, processing purposes, and data subject rights. Client-facing privacy notices [DOC:B8] and end-customer terms [DOC:F2] incorporate these disclosures by reference. Where international data protection laws impose additional transparency obligations (see [DOC:B8] Part II), such requirements are addressed through the International Data Protection Addendum.
