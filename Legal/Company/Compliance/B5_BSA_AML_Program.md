## SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1. This Program establishes the [TERM:BSA_AML_Program] Bank Secrecy Act and Anti-Money Laundering framework for [TERM:Company] CCASH MONEY SERVICES (US) SERIES LLC and all [TERM:Series], including [TERM:ClientSeries] client Series C-###.

### 2.5. BSA/AML Officer Succession

<!-- schema:flow FLOW:BSA_Officer_Succession -->

2.5.1. **Interim Designation.** In the event of BSA/AML Officer (CCO) vacancy, incapacity, or unavailability, the [TERM:Manager] shall designate an interim BSA/AML Officer within seventy-two (72) hours per [DOC:B3] Section 6.6. Until such designation, the CEO shall serve as interim BSA/AML Officer with full authority to execute Program responsibilities.

2.5.2. **Qualification Requirements.** The permanent or interim BSA/AML Officer shall meet qualification standards per [DOC:B9] Section 2.5, including demonstrated knowledge of 31 CFR Part 1022, FinCEN regulations, and OFAC sanctions requirements. CAMS certification and three (3) years BSA/AML experience are preferred per B9 but not mandatory pending permanent appointment.

2.5.3. **FinCEN Notification.** Upon change in the BSA/AML Officer, the [TERM:Company] shall update its FinCEN MSB registration (Form 107) within thirty (30) days if the BSA/AML Officer is also a principal owner, responsible person, or control person under 31 CFR §1022.380(b)(3) [SOURCE:31_cfr_1022.380_msb_registration.txt]. Note: 31 CFR §1022.380 governs registration updates for owners/control persons; BSA/AML Officer designation changes under AML program requirements (31 CFR §1022.210) do not independently trigger Form 107 updates unless the officer is also a control person.

2.5.4. **Continuity Procedures.** During any transition period:
   (a) SAR filing authority remains with the interim BSA/AML Officer;
   (b) Pending SAR investigations shall continue without interruption;
   (c) Training schedules and independent testing timelines shall be maintained;
   (d) The interim officer shall have immediate access to Program documentation, monitoring systems, and regulatory correspondence;
   (e) All succession events shall be documented with effective dates and authority transfers per [DOC:B4].

2.5.5. **Cross-Reference.** Full succession procedures, including emergency scenarios and multiple vacancy handling, are detailed in [DOC:B3] Officer Rotation and Succession Policy.

## SECTION 3: CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROGRAM
[FLOW:CIP] <!-- Customer Identification Program flow -->

3.1. The [TERM:Company] shall collect and verify customer identity information before establishing a relationship or executing covered transactions.
[OBL:OBL-REG-003] <!-- KYC/CIP requirements -->

3.2. Required data elements include full legal name, date of birth (for natural persons), government identification number, physical address, and contact information. Entities must provide formation documents and beneficial ownership information.

3.3. Identity verification shall use reliable, independent documents, data, or information appropriate to risk, which may include government-issued photo ID, corporate records, database checks, and liveness or biometric verification for higher-risk relationships.

3.4. Records of collected information, verification methods, and results shall be retained for five years after the relationship ends.

[FLOW:CDD_EDD] <!-- Due Diligence flow -->

## SECTION 5: SANCTIONS SCREENING
[FLOW:Sanctions_Screening]

[OBL:OBL-REG-004] <!-- Sanctions compliance -->

## SECTION 6: TRANSACTION MONITORING AND SAR PROCESS
[FLOW:Transaction_Monitoring]
[FLOW:SAR_Filing]

6.1. The [TERM:Company] shall maintain risk-based monitoring for suspicious activity covering fiat and digital asset flows, including structuring, rapid movement, mixing services, chain-hopping, sanctions evasion, and fraud patterns.

6.2. Alerts shall be investigated promptly, with documented findings and decisions.

6.3. **Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) Filing.** The [TERM:CCO] shall file Suspicious Activity Reports for transactions involving or aggregating at least **$2,000** (or **$5,000** for money orders) when the Company knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect the transaction involves funds from illegal activity, is designed to evade reporting requirements, lacks a lawful purpose, or involves use of the Company to facilitate criminal activity, per 31 CFR §1022.320(a)(2). SARs shall be filed within thirty (30) calendar days of initial detection (or sixty days if no suspect is identified and additional investigation is needed). SAR confidentiality is mandatory; disclosure of SAR existence or filing is prohibited.
[OBL:OBL-REG-001] <!-- Regulatory filings -->

6.4. **Currency Transaction Report (CTR) Filing.** Currency Transaction Reports shall be filed for each transaction in currency (cash) exceeding **$10,000**, including multiple transactions aggregated during a single business day that the Company knows (or has reason to know) are by or on behalf of the same person, per 31 CFR §1010.311. CTRs shall be filed within fifteen (15) calendar days of the transaction.

6.5. State-specific reports shall be filed as required by applicable state money transmitter laws.

6.5. Alert handling shall follow a documented triage process: creation, investigation, escalation, decision, documentation, and closure with retention of case files.

6.6. False positives shall be documented with rationale to improve tuning without weakening controls.

## SECTION 8: TRAVEL RULE AND VASP PROCEDURES
[FLOW:Travel_Rule]

8.1. Travel rule data shall include originator name, account or wallet identifier, physical address or national ID, and beneficiary name and account or wallet identifier, to the extent required by applicable law.

8.2. Travel rule data shall be securely transmitted to the beneficiary institution or VASP before or concurrent with transfer execution.

8.3. Counterparty VASPs shall be risk assessed; transfers to unknown or non-compliant VASPs shall be paused pending review or rejected.

8.4. On/off-ramp transactions shall capture and store originator and beneficiary information consistent with travel rule requirements.

8.5. Transfers lacking required data shall be held or rejected until deficiencies are resolved.

## SECTION 15: AI-ENABLED COMPLIANCE SYSTEMS
[FLOW:AI_Compliance]

15.1. The [TERM:Company] uses AI and machine learning systems for customer identification verification, transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, risk scoring, and suspicious activity detection as part of its AI-centric operational philosophy.

15.2. AI-enabled compliance systems operate under the supervision of the [TERM:CCO]. The CCO retains authority over SAR filing decisions, relationship terminations, and other material compliance determinations regardless of AI recommendations.

15.3. AI system alerts are triaged according to risk-based procedures. High-risk alerts receive expedited human review. The CCO may adjust alert thresholds and review requirements based on system performance and regulatory guidance.

15.4. **Annual Model Validation.** AI compliance systems shall undergo formal model validation at least annually, conducted by personnel or external parties independent of the model development team. Validation shall include:

   (a) Review of model methodology, assumptions, and limitations;

   (b) Testing of model performance against historical data and known outcomes;

   (c) Assessment of model stability and consistency over time;

   (d) Comparison of model outputs against baseline or benchmark methods;

   (e) Documentation of validation findings, recommendations, and remediation plans.

15.5. **Bias and Fairness Testing.** AI systems making decisions affecting customers shall be tested at least annually for potential bias across protected characteristics to the extent data is available, including:

   (a) Analysis of decision outcomes across demographic groups where data permits;

   (b) Review of training data for representational bias;

   (c) Assessment of model features for proxies of protected characteristics;

   (d) Documentation of testing methodology, findings, and any remediation measures.

15.6. **AI System Failsafe and Fallback Procedures.** The [TERM:Company] maintains documented procedures for compliance operations in the event of AI system failure, degradation, or unavailability:
[DOC:B6] <!-- IT Security, Incident, BCP/DRP -->

   (a) **Immediate Failover:** Critical compliance functions (sanctions screening, transaction hold authority) shall have automated failover to rule-based backup systems that can operate independently of AI models;

   (b) **Manual Override:** The CCO and designated compliance personnel retain authority to override, pause, or bypass AI systems at any time without requiring system approval;

   (c) **Degraded Mode Operations:** Procedures for operating in degraded mode with reduced AI capability, including enhanced human review, reduced transaction limits, and elevated escalation thresholds;

   (d) **System Recovery:** Procedures for validating AI system integrity before returning to normal operations after an outage or failure;

   (e) **Testing:** Failover and fallback procedures shall be tested at least annually as part of business continuity testing.

15.7. **AI Decision Accountability.** Notwithstanding the use of AI systems:

   (a) The [TERM:Company], not its AI systems, is responsible for compliance decisions and outcomes;

   (b) AI system outputs are recommendations subject to human judgment; final accountability rests with the responsible [TERM:Officer];

   (c) Clients and [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers may not assert claims against the [TERM:Company] based solely on AI system malfunction if the [TERM:Company] followed documented fallback procedures and exercised reasonable human oversight;

   (d) The [TERM:Company] shall not disclaim liability to regulators based on AI system error; regulatory responsibility remains with the [TERM:Company] and its [TERM:Officers].

15.8. Training for compliance personnel includes AI system operation, interpretation of AI outputs, override procedures, and escalation protocols.

15.9. As AI technology demonstrates reliability in specific compliance domains, the [TERM:Company] may authorize expanded autonomous decision-making for routine, low-risk determinations, subject to CCO approval, documented criteria, and ongoing monitoring.

15.10. Clients and [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers consent to AI-enabled compliance processing as a condition of service. The [TERM:Company] discloses AI involvement in compliance decisions where required by applicable law or regulation.

15.11. **AI Explainability and Audit Trail.** AI compliance systems shall maintain sufficient documentation to explain decision rationale:

   (a) **Decision Logging:** All AI-generated compliance decisions (approvals, denials, escalations, risk scores) shall be logged with timestamp, input data summary, model version, and output rationale;

   (b) **Explainability:** For decisions adversely affecting customers (account closures, transaction holds, relationship terminations), the [TERM:Company] shall be able to articulate the primary factors contributing to the AI recommendation in human-understandable terms;

   (c) **Audit Trail Retention:** AI decision logs shall be retained for the same period as underlying transaction records per [DOC:B4];

   (d) **Regulatory Access:** AI decision logs and model documentation shall be made available to regulators upon examination request.

15.12. **Regulatory Engagement on AI.** The [TERM:CCO] shall monitor regulatory developments regarding AI in financial services and:

   (a) Maintain awareness of FinCEN, state regulator, and federal prudential regulator guidance on AI/ML in BSA/AML compliance;

   (b) Participate in industry working groups and comment processes on AI regulation where appropriate;

   (c) Proactively engage with examiners regarding the [TERM:Company]'s AI compliance approach during examinations;

   (d) Document and remediate any examiner concerns regarding AI system usage within 90 days of examination report.

15.13. **Performance Monitoring.** AI compliance systems shall be subject to ongoing performance monitoring including detection rates, false positive rates, processing times, and system availability. Significant performance degradation shall be escalated to the CCO and [TERM:CTO] for remediation.

15.14. **Regulatory Compliance.** AI systems shall be designed and operated in compliance with applicable regulatory guidance on model risk management, including OCC 2011-12 and SR 11-7 where applicable, and emerging AI-specific regulatory requirements.
