## ARTICLE 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

**1.1 Purpose**

This Recordkeeping Policy establishes requirements for creating, maintaining, retaining, and disposing of records for Sails DAO LLC ("**Company**") and all Series Issuers. Proper recordkeeping is essential for:

- Regulatory compliance (SEC, state securities, IRS, FinCEN)
- Legal defense and litigation support
- Business continuity and disaster recovery
- Investor transparency and accountability
- Operational efficiency

**1.2 Scope**

This Policy applies to:

- All Company records (corporate, financial, legal, operational)
- All Series Issuer records
- Records in all formats (electronic, paper, audio, video)
- Records created, received, or maintained by Company, employees, contractors, and agents

**1.3 Definitions**

- **Record:** Any information created, received, or maintained as evidence of business activities
- **Retention Period:** Minimum time a record must be retained
- **Legal Hold:** Suspension of normal retention/destruction due to litigation or investigation
- **Destruction:** Secure disposal of records after retention period expires

## ARTICLE 3: RECORD MANAGEMENT PROCEDURES

**3.1 Record Creation**

**(a) Documentation Standards:**
- Records must be accurate, complete, and legible
- Include date, author/creator, and version (where applicable)
- Use standard naming conventions: [Category]_[Description]_[Date]_v[Version]
- Electronic records in standard formats (PDF, DOCX, XLSX)

**(b) Authentication:**
- Material records require appropriate signatures or approvals
- Electronic signatures per E-SIGN Act requirements
- Maintain chain of custody for legally significant records

**3.2 Record Storage**

**(a) Electronic Records:**
- Store in designated document management system
- Apply appropriate access controls (RBAC)
- Enable version control for documents subject to amendment
- Encrypt sensitive data (AES-256 at rest)
- Maintain backups per disaster recovery plan

**(b) Paper Records:**
- Store in secure, climate-controlled facilities
- Apply access controls (locked cabinets/rooms)
- Index and catalog for retrieval
- Minimize paper; prefer electronic records

**(c) Cloud Storage:**
- Use approved cloud providers with SOC 2 certification
- Enable encryption and access controls
- Ensure data residency compliance (for GDPR)
- Maintain vendor agreements with appropriate terms

**3.3 Record Retrieval**

**(a) Access:**
- Grant access based on job function (need-to-know)
- Log access to sensitive records
- Respond to retrieval requests within 24 hours (business records)
- Maintain retrieval procedures for archived records

**(b) Legal/Regulatory Requests:**
- Route through General Counsel
- Preserve responsive records immediately
- Respond within required timeframes
- Document production and retention

**3.4 Record Retention**

**(a) Retention Schedule:**
- Follow retention periods in Article 2
- Begin retention period from trigger date (e.g., transaction date, account closure, offering termination)
- Extend retention if litigation, investigation, or legal hold applies

**(b) Retention Review:**
- Annual review of retention schedule for legal changes
- Quarterly review of records approaching destruction eligibility
- Update retention periods as laws change

**3.5 Record Destruction**

**(a) Destruction Eligibility:**
- Record has met minimum retention period
- No legal hold applies
- No pending litigation, investigation, or audit
- Destruction approved by Records Manager

**(b) Destruction Methods:**
- **Paper:** Cross-cut shredding (DIN 66399 Level P-4 minimum)
- **Electronic:** Secure deletion with overwrite (NIST 800-88 compliant)
- **Media:** Physical destruction or degaussing

**(c) Destruction Documentation:**
- Maintain destruction log (record type, date, method, approver)
- Retain destruction log permanently
- Do NOT destroy destruction logs

**(d) Destruction Prohibition:**
- Never destroy records subject to legal hold
- Never destroy records if litigation is reasonably anticipated
- Destruction during investigation = obstruction of justice

## ARTICLE 4: LEGAL HOLDS

**4.1 Legal Hold Triggers**

Legal hold required when:

- Litigation filed or threatened
- Regulatory investigation commenced or anticipated
- Subpoena or document request received
- Internal investigation initiated
- Any event suggesting future legal proceedings

**4.2 Legal Hold Procedure**

**(a) Initiation:**
- General Counsel initiates legal hold
- Define scope (record types, custodians, date ranges)
- Issue written legal hold notice to all custodians

**(b) Legal Hold Notice:**
- Describe matter requiring preservation
- Identify categories of records to preserve
- Prohibit destruction, alteration, or deletion
- Require acknowledgment from custodians
- Provide contact for questions

**(c) Custodian Responsibilities:**
- Immediately cease destruction of relevant records
- Identify and preserve all responsive records
- Disable auto-delete functions
- Notify Records Manager of relevant records
- Acknowledge receipt of legal hold

**(d) IT Responsibilities:**
- Suspend automated retention/deletion for relevant records
- Preserve relevant backups
- Collect and preserve relevant electronic records
- Maintain chain of custody

**(e) Monitoring:**
- Periodic reminders to custodians
- Verify preservation compliance
- Update scope as matter evolves

**4.3 Legal Hold Release**

**(a) Release Criteria:**
- Matter resolved (litigation concluded, investigation closed)
- Statute of limitations expired
- General Counsel determines preservation no longer required

**(b) Release Procedure:**
- General Counsel issues written release
- Records resume normal retention schedule
- Destruction may proceed for records past retention period
- Document release in legal hold file

- Series designation on all Series documents

- Series dissolution documentation
- Retain records per normal retention periods (do not destroy early)
