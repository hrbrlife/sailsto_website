## SECTION 2: PROHIBITED CLIENTS AND USES
[FLOW:Prohibited_Use_Screening]

2.1. Clients or transactions involving sanctioned persons, entities, or jurisdictions are prohibited.
[OBL:OBL-REG-004] <!-- Sanctions compliance -->

2.2. Darknet markets, mixers, tumblers, and privacy-enhanced tools intended to obscure source or destination of funds are prohibited.

2.3. Money services businesses operating illegally without required licenses in their jurisdiction are prohibited. This does not prohibit clients operating lawfully in jurisdictions where no license is required, or [TERM:ClientSeries] operating as independent Montana MSBs using [TERM:Company] infrastructure.

2.4. Cannabis-related businesses, including cultivation, processing, dispensaries, and ancillary services, are prohibited regardless of state or local legality.

2.5. [TERM:ClientSeries] may not provide cryptocurrency exchange, custody, or wallet services downstream to other money services businesses or virtual asset service providers. Exchange and custody services are for the [TERM:ClientSeries] and its [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers only.

2.6. The [TERM:CCO] may decline unsupported or high-risk jurisdictions and industries where risk cannot be adequately mitigated, with documented risk assessment.

2.7. Fraudulent, deceptive, or abusive practices are prohibited.

## SECTION 3: ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE TRIGGERS
[FLOW:CDD_EDD]

3.1. The following client types and transaction patterns require enhanced due diligence prior to onboarding and on an ongoing basis:
[DOC:B5] <!-- BSA/AML Program EDD requirements -->
[DOC:B9] <!-- Fit and Proper Screening -->

3.1.1. Politically exposed persons and their close associates.

3.1.2. Complex or opaque ownership structures.

3.1.3. Cross-border flows from higher-risk jurisdictions.

3.1.4. High-volume virtual asset activity.

3.1.5. Adverse media concerning the client or its principals.

3.1.6. Licensed gambling operators, including casinos, sportsbooks, online gaming platforms, and lottery services, where the operator holds valid gaming licenses in all jurisdictions of operation.

3.2. Enhanced due diligence includes source of funds, source of wealth, ownership verification, adverse media review, and senior approval prior to onboarding.

3.3. For licensed gambling operators, enhanced due diligence also includes verification of gaming licenses, review of the operator's own AML program, transaction monitoring with gambling-specific thresholds, and periodic license status verification.

## SECTION 6: TRANSPARENCY AND INFORMATION SHARING

6.1. The [TERM:Company] maintains transparent relationships with regulators, banking partners, correspondent institutions, payment processors, and other business partners.
[DOC:B5] <!-- BSA/AML information sharing -->

6.2. Clients consent to disclosure of compliance information to the [TERM:Company]'s business partners and regulators as follows.

6.2.1. Legal requirement means disclosure required by law, regulation, subpoena, or court order.

6.2.2. Contractual requirement means disclosure required by banking agreements, correspondent contracts, or payment processor agreements.

6.2.3. Risk-triggered disclosure means higher-risk indicators may trigger expedited disclosure to banking partners.

6.3. Higher-risk indicators that may trigger disclosure include enhanced due diligence categories, suspicious activity indicators, sanctions screening alerts, adverse media, unusual transaction patterns, and regulatory inquiries.

6.4. The [TERM:CCO] determines the scope and timing of disclosures, balancing regulatory obligations, contractual requirements, and privacy considerations.

6.5. **Consent Requirement.** Clients shall provide affirmative consent to this information sharing framework during onboarding through the consent mechanisms established in [DOC:F1]. Such consent shall:
(a) Be obtained through clear, affirmative action (e.g., checkbox acknowledgment, electronic signature);
(b) Be documented and retained per [DOC:B4];
(c) Clearly identify the categories of information that may be shared;
(d) Identify the categories of recipients (regulators, banking partners, law enforcement);
(e) Explain the legal bases for mandatory disclosures versus discretionary disclosures;
(f) Provide a withdrawal and opt-out mechanism, at least as easy as providing consent, as described in [DOC:B8] and [DOC:F1].

6.5.1. Continued use of services without affirmative consent does not constitute valid consent under applicable data protection laws.

## SECTION 9: GEOGRAPHIC AND CROSS-BORDER CLIENT REQUIREMENTS
[FLOW:Geographic_Compliance]

9.1. **Client Local Law Covenant.** Each client covenants and represents that:
[OBL:OBL-CLIENT-REG-001] <!-- Client licensing obligations -->

   (a) The client has obtained, or has determined in good faith that no license is required for, all licenses, registrations, and regulatory approvals required for the client's business activities in each jurisdiction where the client operates;

   (b) The client's use of [TERM:Company] services complies with all applicable laws, regulations, and regulatory guidance in each jurisdiction where the client or its [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers are located;

   (c) The client shall promptly notify the [TERM:Company] of any material change in regulatory requirements, licensing status, or legal developments that affect the client's authority to conduct its business or use [TERM:Company] services.

9.2. **Geographic Restrictions.** The [TERM:Company] may restrict services to or from certain geographic areas based on:

   (a) Sanctions or embargo requirements;

   (b) Regulatory constraints or lack of required licenses;

   (c) Banking partner or payment processor restrictions;

   (d) Elevated risk assessments; or

   (e) Inability to adequately verify customer identity or conduct required due diligence.

9.3. **Notification of Geographic Restrictions.** The [TERM:Company] shall maintain and provide to clients upon request a current list of restricted jurisdictions. Changes to restricted jurisdictions shall be communicated to affected clients promptly.

9.4. **Cross-Border Transaction Requirements.** For transactions involving jurisdictions outside the United States:

   (a) Clients shall provide sufficient information for the [TERM:Company] to assess jurisdiction-specific risks and regulatory requirements;

   (b) Enhanced due diligence may apply to cross-border transactions based on destination or origination jurisdiction;

   (c) Additional documentation, transaction limits, or processing delays may apply.

9.5. **Regulatory Change Escape.** If legal or regulatory changes in a client's jurisdiction of operation render the client's use of [TERM:Company] services unlawful, impractical, or materially more burdensome:

   (a) The [TERM:Company] may restrict or modify services to that jurisdiction with thirty days' notice (or immediately where required for legal compliance);

   (b) The client may terminate without penalty if the restriction materially impairs the client's intended use of services;

   (c) Neither party shall be liable to the other for costs, losses, or damages arising from regulatory changes beyond that party's control.

9.6. **No Cross-Border Guarantees.** The [TERM:Company] makes no representation that its services are suitable for use in all jurisdictions. Clients are responsible for determining the legality of their use of [TERM:Company] services in each jurisdiction where they operate.

### 9.7. Montana MSB Operating Scope

9.7.1. Each [TERM:ClientSeries] operates as an independent Montana MSB. Montana's MSB registration permits money transmission within Montana without additional state licensing.

9.7.2. For transactions involving persons located outside Montana, [TERM:ClientSeries] must use one of the approved methods set forth in this Section.

### 9.8. Cryptocurrency-Exempt States

9.8.1. Certain states do not require money transmitter licenses for cryptocurrency transactions or have exemptions that may apply. The [TERM:Company] maintains a current list of states where cryptocurrency-only activities may be conducted without additional licensing. See [DOC:N3] Approved States Schedule, Section 3 (Crypto-Exempt Jurisdictions) for the authoritative list.

9.8.2. [TERM:ClientSeries] may serve [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers in Crypto-Exempt States for cryptocurrency transactions only, subject to compliance with all other requirements of this Policy.

9.8.3. The Crypto-Exempt States list within [DOC:N3] is maintained by the [TERM:CCO] and updated as state laws change. Current exempt states and applicable statutory citations are subject to legal review and verification before reliance.

### 9.9. Partner MSB Interstate Arrangements
[FLOW:Partner_MSB_Routing]

9.9.1. For fiat currency transmission to or from persons located outside Montana and outside Crypto-Exempt States, the [TERM:Company] may route transactions through Partner MSBs that hold appropriate licenses in the destination or origination state.

9.9.2. Partner MSBs are independently licensed money transmitters that have entered into routing agreements with the [TERM:Company]. The [TERM:Company] conducts due diligence on Partner MSBs before entering routing arrangements.

9.9.3. Transactions routed through Partner MSBs are subject to additional fees as set forth in the [DOC:C3] Pricing and Routing Schedule.

9.9.4. [TERM:ClientSeries] and their [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers consent to Partner MSB routing as a condition of service for interstate fiat transactions.

### 9.10. Prohibited Geographic Activities

9.10.1. [TERM:ClientSeries] shall not conduct money transmission in states where neither the [TERM:Company] nor a Partner MSB holds required licenses, except for transactions that qualify for applicable exemptions.

9.10.2. [TERM:ClientSeries] shall not conduct any activities involving persons or entities located in OFAC-sanctioned countries or territories. All transactions shall be screened against OFAC sanctions lists (SDN, SSI, and country programs) in real-time per [DOC:B5] Section 5. Blocked transactions shall be reported to OFAC per 31 CFR Part 501.
[OBL:OBL-REG-004]

9.10.3. The [TERM:Company] maintains a Prohibited Jurisdictions List, updated as sanctions and licensing conditions change. This list incorporates OFAC country programs, comprehensive sanctions (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and Crimea region), and secondary sanctions considerations.

### 9.11. Geographic Verification Requirements

9.11.1. [TERM:ClientSeries] are responsible for verifying the geographic location of their [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers at onboarding and for high-risk transactions.

9.11.2. The [TERM:Company] implements technical controls including IP geolocation, address verification, and other measures to support geographic compliance.

9.11.3. [TERM:EndCustomer] End-customers using VPNs, proxies, or other tools to mask their location may be subject to enhanced verification or service restrictions.

### 9.12. State Expansion Protocol

9.12.1. The [TERM:Company] may expand its direct licensing footprint or Partner MSB network over time. [TERM:ClientSeries] will be notified of newly available states.

9.12.2. [TERM:ClientSeries] shall not represent that services are available in states not yet authorized without [TERM:Company] approval.

## SECTION 10: COMPLIANCE ENFORCEMENT AND ESCALATION
[FLOW:Compliance_Enforcement]

10.1. The [TERM:Company] enforces compliance requirements proportionately, with warnings and remediation opportunities before service restrictions where circumstances permit.

10.2. The enforcement escalation process proceeds as follows, unless immediate action is required for regulatory, sanctions, or safety reasons.

10.2.1. Warning means the [TERM:Company] notifies the client of the compliance concern in writing and requests remediation within a specified timeframe, typically seven days.

10.2.2. Remediation period means the client has an opportunity to address the concern, provide information, or adjust practices. The [TERM:Company] provides reasonable cooperation during this period.

10.2.3. Soft restrictions mean if remediation is not satisfactory, the [TERM:Company] may implement proportionate restrictions such as user interface friction, processing delays, enhanced review requirements, or reduced transaction limits.

10.2.4. Hard restrictions mean if concerns persist, the [TERM:Company] may implement technical blocks including geographic IP restrictions, transaction rejection, API access suspension, or account freezes.

10.2.5. Termination means if the client fails to remediate or the violation is severe, the [TERM:Company] may terminate the client relationship in accordance with the [DOC:F1] Client Services Agreement.

10.3. For regulatory requirements, sanctions violations, fraud, or safety concerns, the [TERM:Company] may implement immediate restrictions or termination without prior warning or escalation.

10.4. The [TERM:Company] implements technical controls to support compliance enforcement, including geographic IP blocking, transaction velocity limits, user interface warnings, and automated transaction screening. These controls apply to [TERM:ClientSeries] and their [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers.

10.5. Clients consent to technical enforcement measures as a condition of service. The [TERM:Company] shall inform clients of enforcement actions promptly, except where disclosure is prohibited by law or would compromise an investigation.
