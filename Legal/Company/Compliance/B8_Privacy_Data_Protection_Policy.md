## SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1. This Policy sets privacy and data protection standards for all [TERM:Series], covering personal data handled for onboarding, transactions, compliance, and support.

1.2. This Policy applies to personal data processing regardless of data subject location and aligns with applicable data protection laws, including but not limited to U.S. federal and state privacy laws and, where triggered by the conditions in Section 10, international data protection requirements.

1.3. **Two-Tier Structure.** Sections 1 through 9 establish baseline privacy protections that apply to all data subjects regardless of location. Section 10 and following establish the International Data Protection Addendum, which provides enhanced protections triggered when:
   (a) The data subject is located in the European Economic Area, United Kingdom, or Switzerland at the time of data collection;
   (b) The [TERM:Company] or a Client specifically targets individuals in jurisdictions with comprehensive data protection laws; or
   (c) Personal data is transferred to or from such jurisdictions.

## SECTION 2: DATA CONTROLLER AND PROCESSOR ROLES
[FLOW:Data_Roles]

2.1. **Controller Activities.** The [TERM:Company] acts as a data controller for:
   (a) Personal data of its own employees, officers, and contractors;
   (b) Client KYC/KYB data collected for the [TERM:Company]'s own compliance obligations;
   (c) Data processed to fulfill the [TERM:Company]'s independent legal obligations (SAR filing, sanctions screening, regulatory reporting).

2.2. **Processor Activities.** The [TERM:Company] acts as a data processor for:
   (a) [TERM:EndCustomer] End-customer data processed on behalf of Clients for transaction execution and Client's commercial operations;
   (b) Client business data where the Client determines the purposes and means of processing.

2.3. **Joint Controller Activities.** The [TERM:Company] and Clients act as joint controllers for:
   (a) AML/KYC data where both parties have independent legal obligations;
   (b) Transaction monitoring data where both the [TERM:Company] and Client have compliance responsibilities.

2.4. Written agreements establishing controller/processor relationships shall be maintained per [DOC:F1] and this Policy.

[FLOW:Data_Processing]

3.4.1. Legal obligation means applicable anti-money laundering requirements mandate collection, verification, retention, and disclosure of customer identification and transaction information.

3.4.2. Legitimate interests means prevention of financial crime and maintenance of banking relationships constitute legitimate interests given the regulated nature of money services business activities.

3.5. For cross-border data transfers to banking partners, correspondents, and regulators, the [TERM:Company] relies on appropriate legal mechanisms as required by applicable data protection laws.

## SECTION 8: THIRD-PARTY PROCESSORS AND BUSINESS PARTNER DISCLOSURE

8.1. Processors handling personal data shall be vetted for security and privacy capability and bound by written agreements with confidentiality, security, and breach notification terms.

8.2. Compliance information may be disclosed to banking partners, correspondent institutions, payment processors, and regulators as follows.

8.2.1. Legal requirement means disclosure required by law, regulation, subpoena, or court order.

8.2.2. Contractual requirement means disclosure required by banking agreements, correspondent contracts, or payment processor agreements.

8.2.3. Risk-triggered disclosure means higher-risk indicators may trigger expedited disclosure to banking partners.
[DOC:B7] <!-- Acceptable Client Use Policy -->

8.3. The [TERM:CCO] maintains a register of recipients with access to compliance data, the categories of data shared, and the legal or contractual basis for sharing.

8.4. **Consent and Legal Basis for Disclosures.**
   (a) **Compliance-Required Disclosures**: Disclosures required for legal obligations (e.g., SAR filing, regulatory examination responses, court orders) are made under legal obligation basis without requiring separate consent. Data subjects shall be informed of such disclosures except where prohibited by law.
   (b) **Contractual Disclosures**: Disclosures necessary for contract performance (e.g., payment processing, banking relationships) are made under contract performance basis as disclosed in service agreements.
   (c) **Consent-Based Disclosures**: For disclosures not required by law or contract (e.g., marketing, analytics sharing), the [TERM:Company] shall obtain explicit, granular, freely-given consent documented with timestamp, scope, and withdrawal mechanism.
   (d) **Withdrawal of Consent**: Where consent is the legal basis, data subjects may withdraw consent at any time via written notice to the DPO or through account settings. Withdrawal does not affect lawfulness of prior processing.
   (e) **U.S. State Privacy Law Opt-Outs**: For residents of states with comprehensive privacy laws (including California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, and Utah), the [TERM:Company] provides opt-out mechanisms for sale or sharing of personal information as defined by applicable state law. Disclosures for compliance purposes are not "sales" under such laws.
   (f) **Onboarding Disclosure**: Clients and their [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers are informed of this disclosure framework during onboarding via [DOC:F1] and [DOC:F2] respectively.

## SECTION 11: INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS
[FLOW:Cross_Border_Transfer]

11.1. **Trigger Conditions.** This Addendum applies when:
   (a) The data subject is located in the European Economic Area ("EEA"), United Kingdom, or Switzerland at the time of data collection;
   (b) The [TERM:Company] or a Client specifically targets individuals in jurisdictions with comprehensive data protection laws through marketing, localized services, or local language offerings; or
   (c) Personal data is transferred from covered jurisdictions to the United States or other jurisdictions.

11.2. **Transfer Mechanisms.** When personal data is transferred from the EEA, United Kingdom, or Switzerland to the United States or other jurisdictions, the [TERM:Company] shall rely on one or more of the following legal mechanisms:

   (a) **Standard Contractual Clauses ("SCCs"):** The [TERM:Company] shall execute the Standard Contractual Clauses approved by relevant data protection authorities with data exporters or importers as appropriate. The [TERM:Company] maintains template SCCs and shall incorporate them into relevant agreements;

   (b) **UK International Data Transfer Agreement or Addendum:** For transfers from the United Kingdom, the [TERM:Company] shall use the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs, as appropriate;

   (c) **Swiss Data Protection Clauses:** For transfers from Switzerland, the [TERM:Company] shall use transfer mechanisms recognized by the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner;

   (d) **Other Approved Mechanisms:** Where available, the [TERM:Company] may rely on binding corporate rules, adequacy decisions, or other legally approved transfer mechanisms.

11.3. **EEA Representative.** If the [TERM:Company] processes personal data of data subjects located in the EEA and is not established in the EEA, the [TERM:Company] shall appoint a representative in the European Union as required by applicable data protection law. The representative's contact details shall be provided in privacy notices and made available upon request.

11.4. **UK Representative.** If the [TERM:Company] processes personal data of data subjects located in the United Kingdom and is not established in the UK, the [TERM:Company] shall appoint a representative in the United Kingdom as required by applicable data protection law. The representative's contact details shall be provided in privacy notices and made available upon request.

11.5. **Transfer Impact Assessments.** For transfers to jurisdictions with heightened data protection risks, the [TERM:Company] shall conduct transfer impact assessments to evaluate whether additional safeguards are necessary to ensure equivalent protection.

11.6. **Client Responsibilities.** Clients transferring personal data to the [TERM:Company] are responsible for ensuring they have a lawful basis for such transfer and for providing required notices to data subjects. Clients shall execute any data processing agreements or transfer mechanisms required by applicable law.

## SECTION 12: ENHANCED DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS (INTERNATIONAL)

12.1. **Scope.** When this Addendum applies, data subjects have the following enhanced rights in addition to the baseline rights in Section 4:

12.2. **Right to Access.** Data subjects may obtain confirmation of processing, a copy of their personal data, and information about processing purposes, categories, recipients, retention periods, and source of data.

12.3. **Right to Rectification.** Data subjects may request correction of inaccurate data and completion of incomplete data.

12.4. **Right to Erasure.** Data subjects may request deletion of personal data where: (a) no longer necessary for original purpose; (b) consent withdrawn; (c) data unlawfully processed; or (d) required by law. This right is subject to exceptions for legal obligation, public interest, legal claims, and BSA/AML retention requirements.

12.5. **Right to Restriction.** Data subjects may request restriction of processing in certain circumstances, including where accuracy is contested or processing is unlawful.

12.6. **Right to Data Portability.** Data subjects may receive their data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format where processing is based on consent or contract and carried out by automated means.

12.7. **Right to Object.** Data subjects may object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing purposes.

12.8. **Rights Regarding Automated Decisions.** Data subjects may request human intervention, express their point of view, and contest decisions made solely by automated processing that produce legal or similarly significant effects.

12.9. **Response Timeline.** The [TERM:Company] shall respond to requests within thirty (30) days, extendable by an additional sixty (60) days for complex requests with notification to the data subject.

12.10. **Exceptions.** Rights may be limited where exercise would: (a) prejudice BSA/AML compliance or SAR confidentiality; (b) interfere with regulatory examinations; (c) compromise ongoing investigations; or (d) conflict with legal retention obligations. Limitations shall be documented with specific legal basis.

## SECTION 13: GEOGRAPHIC RESTRICTIONS AND COMPLIANCE

13.1. **Prohibited Jurisdictions.** The [TERM:Company] does not knowingly process personal data of, or provide services to, individuals located in jurisdictions subject to comprehensive U.S. sanctions or where the [TERM:Company]'s services would violate local law. Clients are responsible for ensuring their [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers are not located in prohibited jurisdictions.
[DOC:B7] <!-- Acceptable Client Use Policy -->

13.2. **Client Local Law Compliance.** Clients operating in jurisdictions outside the United States are responsible for compliance with local data protection laws applicable to their activities. The [TERM:Company] provides services from the United States and does not guarantee compliance with all local laws in client jurisdictions.

13.3. **Regulatory Change Provisions.** If changes in data protection law, regulatory guidance, or international transfer mechanisms materially affect the [TERM:Company]'s ability to process personal data for a client or its [TERM:EndCustomer] end-customers, the [TERM:Company] may:

   (a) Implement additional safeguards or transfer mechanisms as necessary;

   (b) Restrict services to certain jurisdictions or data subjects;

   (c) Provide notice and work with the client to develop a compliant alternative; or

   (d) If no compliant alternative is reasonably available, terminate services for affected data processing activities with sixty (60) days' notice.

13.4. **No Regulatory Opinion.** Nothing in this Policy constitutes legal advice or a representation that the [TERM:Company]'s data protection practices comply with all laws applicable to clients in their jurisdictions. Clients should obtain independent legal advice regarding their data protection obligations.

## SECTION 14: JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

14.1. **Purpose.** This section provides jurisdiction-specific privacy requirements that apply in addition to baseline and international addendum provisions.

14.2. **California (CCPA/CPRA).**
   (a) California residents have rights to know, access, delete, correct, and opt-out of sale/sharing;
   (b) The [TERM:Company] does not sell personal information as defined by California law;
   (c) Opt-out mechanism: written request to DPO or account settings;
   (d) Non-discrimination for exercising privacy rights.

14.3. **Other U.S. State Privacy Laws.** Residents of Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, and other states with comprehensive privacy laws have similar rights as provided by applicable state statutes.

14.4. **European Economic Area, United Kingdom, and Switzerland.** Enhanced rights per Section 12; transfer mechanisms per Section 11; local representatives per Sections 11.3-11.4.

14.5. **Other Jurisdictions.** For jurisdictions not specifically addressed, the [TERM:Company] applies baseline protections and cooperates with Clients to implement jurisdiction-specific requirements as part of the Client relationship.
