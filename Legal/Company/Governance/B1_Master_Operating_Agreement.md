## ARTICLE 4: SERIES AUTHORIZATION

4.1. The Company may establish multiple Series pursuant to Montana Code Annotated Section 35-8-304. Each Series may have separate members, separate managers, separate assets and liabilities, separate business purposes, and its own operating provisions.

4.2. The debts, liabilities, and obligations of each Series are enforceable only against the assets of that Series. The debts, liabilities, and obligations of any Series are not enforceable against the assets of any other Series, the assets of the Company generally, or the personal assets of any Member or Manager.

4.3. Each Series shall maintain separate books, records, and accounts to preserve liability separation between Series [OBL:RECORD-SERIES-001].

4.4. The initial Series are established as set forth in Exhibit A [DOC:A2] to the Articles. Series T-1 is the Treasury and Payment Services Series, which handles fiat payment operations. Series T-2 is the Crypto Custody Series, which handles digital asset custody. Series T-3 is the Operational Expenses Series, which handles operating expenses. Series B-2 is the Platform IP Series, which holds software, patents, and other intellectual property. Series P-1 is the Compliance and Risk Series, which handles regulatory compliance. Series P-2 is the Intragroup Finance Series, which handles internal treasury operations. Series P-3 is the IT Consulting Series, which provides professional services. Series S-1 is the Capital Raising Series, which handles securities offerings.

4.7. 4.8. **Inter-Series Operational Conflicts.** If the operations of one Client Series create a material risk to other Client Series or to the Company’s banking, Partner MSB, custody, or regulatory relationships (including reputational risk, elevated chargeback/fraud rates, or regulator concern), the Manager and Chief Compliance Officer may impose proportionate restrictions on the affected Series, including temporary suspensions, enhanced controls, or offboarding directives, to protect the overall enterprise and other Series. The Company will document the basis for restrictions and will apply consistent standards across similarly situated Series. [OBL:INTERSERIES-CONFLICT-001]

### 14.9. Conflict of Interest and Related-Party Transactions

14.9.1. **Disclosure Requirement.** Any Manager, Officer, or Member who has a direct or indirect personal, financial, or other interest in any transaction or arrangement involving the Company, any Series, or any Client Series must promptly disclose such interest in writing to the Manager (or, if the Manager has the conflict, to all Members).

14.9.2. **Related-Party Transactions Defined.** A "related-party transaction" means any transaction or arrangement between (a) the Company or any Functional Series, on the one hand, and (b) any Client Series, Manager, Officer, Member, or any entity in which a Manager, Officer, or Member has a significant ownership interest or management role, on the other hand. Related-party transactions include but are not limited to: fee arrangements between the Company and Client Series; loans or advances from Series P-2 (Intragroup Finance) to any other Series; service agreements between Functional Series and Client Series; and any assignment of intellectual property, contracts, or business opportunities.

14.9.3. **Arm's-Length Standard.** All related-party transactions shall be conducted on arm's-length terms, meaning terms that are at least as favorable to each party as would be obtainable in a comparable transaction with an unrelated third party.

14.9.4. **Approval Requirements.** Related-party transactions involving amounts exceeding ten thousand dollars ($10,000) or that are outside the ordinary course of business require advance written approval from the Manager (or, if the Manager is conflicted, from Members holding a majority of membership interests). The Pricing and Routing Schedule [DOC:C3] and Client Services Agreement [DOC:F1] fee structures, having been disclosed and agreed upon by clients, are deemed pre-approved for purposes of this Section 14.9.4.

14.9.5. **Documentation.** All related-party transactions shall be documented in writing, including the parties involved, the nature and terms of the transaction, the business purpose, and any conflict disclosures and approvals.

14.9.6. **Recusal.** Any person with a conflict of interest in a particular transaction shall recuse themselves from voting or decision-making regarding that transaction.

14.9.7. **Annual Review.** The Chief Compliance Officer shall conduct an annual review of related-party transactions to ensure compliance with this Section 14.9 [OBL:CONFLICT-REVIEW-001].

14.9.8. **Member Disclosure.** Client Series Members shall be informed of this conflict of interest policy during onboarding and shall have access to information regarding related-party transactions affecting their Series upon reasonable request.

14.10. **Independent Oversight and External Review.** The Company shall maintain independent oversight mechanisms appropriate to its risk profile, including:

14.10.1. **Independent Testing Beyond BSA/AML.** In addition to required independent BSA/AML testing, the Manager may commission independent reviews of controls (e.g., cybersecurity, vendor risk, funds safeguarding, and compliance operations) as warranted by risk, banking partner expectations, or regulatory feedback. [OBL:OVERSIGHT-001]

14.10.2. **Independent Advisor (Optional).** The Manager may appoint an independent compliance or risk advisor (not an employee or Officer) to review the Company’s control environment and provide written recommendations at least annually.

## ARTICLE 16: SERIES TERMINATION AND DISSOLUTION

### 16.1. Series Termination Events

16.1.1. A Series (including any Client Series [TERM:Client Series]) may be dissolved and terminated upon the occurrence of any of the following events:

   (a) Written request by the Series Member(s) holding a majority of Series interests, subject to Manager approval;
   
   (b) Termination of the Client Services Agreement [DOC:F1] pursuant to its terms, for Client Series;
   
   (c) Material breach by the Series Member of this Agreement, the Series Designation, or the Client Services Agreement that remains uncured for thirty (30) days after written notice;
   
   (d) Revocation, suspension, or non-renewal of the Series's FinCEN MSB registration [OBL:FINCEN-REG-001];
   
   (e) Determination by the Manager or Chief Compliance Officer that the Series poses unacceptable compliance or reputational risk to the Company [DECISION:COMPLIANCE-001];
   
   (f) Bankruptcy, insolvency, or dissolution of the Series Member (for single-member Series);
   
   (g) Written decision by the Manager, upon ninety (90) days prior written notice to the Series Member(s), for any reason or no reason;
   
   (h) Any other event specified in the Series Designation.

### 16.2. Termination Procedures

16.2.1. **Notice Requirements.** Upon the occurrence of a termination event under Section 16.1, the Manager shall provide written notice to the Series Member(s) specifying the termination event, the effective termination date (which shall be not less than thirty (30) days from notice except for termination under Section 16.1.1(d) or (e), which may be immediate), the wind-down procedures to be followed, and the deadline for compliance with wind-down requirements.

16.2.2. **Immediate Termination.** Notwithstanding Section 16.2.1, the Manager may immediately terminate a Series without prior notice if required by law, regulation, or order of a governmental authority; if the Series is involved in suspected fraud, money laundering, terrorist financing, or sanctions violations; or if continued operation poses imminent risk to the Company, other Series, or banking relationships.

### 16.3. Wind-Down Procedures

16.3.1. **Cessation of New Business.** Upon notice of termination, the Series shall immediately cease accepting new customers, processing new transactions (except to complete pending transactions or return customer funds), and marketing or soliciting business.

16.3.2. **Customer Fund Return.** For Client Series, all End-Customer [TERM:End Customer] funds must be returned to End-Customers within the time period specified by the Manager, which shall not exceed sixty (60) days except where prevented by regulatory hold, dispute, or investigation. Customer notification procedures shall be coordinated with the Company.

16.3.3. **FinCEN Notification.** The Series Member shall file appropriate notice with FinCEN within one hundred eighty (180) days of cessation of money services business activities, or as otherwise required by law [OBL:FINCEN-UPDATE-001].

16.3.4. **State Filings.** The Manager shall file Articles of Amendment with the Montana Secretary of State to remove the terminated Series from the Company's organizational documents, at the cost of the terminating Series Member or as otherwise agreed.

16.3.5. **Record Retention.** All records of the terminated Series required to be retained under federal or state law, including BSA/AML records, shall be transferred to the Company for retention in accordance with the Record-Keeping Policy [DOC:B4]. The Company shall retain such records for not less than five (5) years following termination.

### 16.4. Asset Distribution and Liability Settlement

16.4.1. **Liability Priority.** Upon termination, the assets of the Series shall be applied in the following order of priority:

   (a) First, to return all End-Customer funds to End-Customers;
   
   (b) Second, to pay all outstanding fees, charges, and amounts owed to the Company or other Series;
   
   (c) Third, to pay all other debts and liabilities of the Series;
   
   (d) Fourth, to establish reserves for contingent or unliquidated liabilities as determined by the Manager;
   
   (e) Fifth, to distribute remaining assets, if any, to the Series Member(s) in accordance with their interests.

16.4.2. **Shortfall.** If Series assets are insufficient to satisfy all liabilities, the Series Member(s) shall remain liable for any shortfall to the extent provided in the Series Designation or Client Services Agreement, subject to the liability limitations of Montana Code Annotated Section 35-8-304.

16.4.3. **Company Offset Rights.** The Company may offset any amounts owed to it by the Series against any amounts distributable to the Series Member(s).

### 16.5. Post-Termination Obligations

16.5.1. The following obligations survive Series termination: confidentiality obligations; indemnification obligations; record retention and cooperation with regulatory inquiries; arbitration provisions; and any other obligations expressly stated to survive.

16.5.2. The Series Member shall cooperate fully with any regulatory examination, investigation, or inquiry relating to the terminated Series for a period of five (5) years following termination.

### 16.6. Involuntary Termination Rights Reserved

16.6.1. The Manager's right to terminate any Series under this Article 16 is absolute and may not be waived except by express written agreement signed by the Manager.

16.6.2. No course of dealing, delay, or forbearance by the Manager shall constitute a waiver of termination rights.

## ARTICLE 18: DISPUTE RESOLUTION

18.1. **Negotiation.** Before initiating arbitration, the parties shall attempt good faith negotiation for thirty days following written notice of the dispute.

### 18.2. Arbitration Requirement

18.2.1. Any dispute, controversy, or claim arising out of or relating to this Agreement, the Company, any Series, or the relationships among Members, the Manager, Officers, or Series, shall be resolved by binding arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association ("AAA") under its Commercial Arbitration Rules.

18.2.2. The arbitration shall be seated in Montana and governed by Montana law, without regard to conflict of laws principles.

18.2.3. The Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §§ 1-16, shall govern the interpretation and enforcement of this arbitration provision.

18.2.4. This arbitration requirement applies to disputes between the Company and any Member, between the Manager and any Member, between any Series and any other Series, between the Company and any client series, and between any client series and the Company's functional series.

### 18.3. AI-Led Arbitration Opt-In

18.3.1. Where the AAA offers AI-assisted or AI-led arbitration procedures (including the AAA AI-Arbitrator tool or successor programs), the parties may mutually elect in writing to use such AI-assisted procedures for eligible disputes.

18.3.2. Election of AI-assisted arbitration requires written consent of both parties to the dispute. If either party declines, or if the dispute type is not eligible under AAA's then-current AI arbitration rules, the dispute shall proceed under traditional AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules with human arbitrators.

18.3.3. In AI-assisted arbitration, the parties acknowledge that AI tools may be used to analyze submissions, generate case summaries, and propose draft awards, subject to review, revision, and final decision by a human AAA arbitrator who retains ultimate decision-making authority.

18.3.4. The Company maintains current information regarding AAA AI arbitration eligibility and availability, which the Chief Compliance Officer shall make available to parties upon request.

18.3.5. The Company's preference for AI-enabled operations extends to dispute resolution. Parties are encouraged to consider AI-assisted arbitration where available as consistent with the Company's technology philosophy.

### 18.4. Arbitration Procedures

18.4.1. The arbitration shall be conducted by a single arbitrator selected in accordance with AAA rules, unless the amount in controversy exceeds one million dollars, in which case a panel of three arbitrators shall be appointed.

18.4.2. Questions of arbitrability, including the scope, validity, and enforceability of this arbitration provision, shall be decided by the arbitrator.

18.4.3. The arbitrator shall have the authority to award any remedy available under applicable law, including injunctive relief and specific performance.

18.4.4. Discovery shall be limited as permitted by the AAA rules, unless the arbitrator determines broader discovery is necessary for a fair resolution.

### 18.5. Confidentiality

18.5.1. All arbitration proceedings, including filings, evidence, testimony, and awards, shall be confidential unless disclosure is required by law or necessary to enforce an award.

### 18.6. Award Finality and Enforcement

18.6.1. The arbitrator's award shall be final and binding on the parties, subject only to the limited grounds for vacatur, modification, or correction under the Federal Arbitration Act.

18.6.2. Judgment on the arbitrator's award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.

### 18.7. Costs and Fees

18.7.1. Each party shall bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, unless the arbitrator determines that a party's claims or defenses were frivolous or brought in bad faith, in which case the arbitrator may award reasonable attorneys' fees to the prevailing party.

18.7.2. AAA filing fees and arbitrator compensation shall be allocated in accordance with AAA rules, subject to reallocation by the arbitrator in the final award.

### 18.8. Class Action Waiver

18.8.1. All disputes shall be arbitrated on an individual basis. Neither party may bring claims as a plaintiff or class member in any purported class, collective, or representative action.

18.8.2. The arbitrator may not consolidate claims of more than one party or preside over any form of class or representative proceeding.

### 18.9. End-Customer Disputes

18.9.1. Each client series shall include an arbitration provision in its end-customer agreements requiring AAA arbitration for disputes between the client series and its end-customers, seated in Montana, governed by Montana law.

18.9.2. Client series end-customer arbitration provisions shall include an opt-in for AI-led arbitration where available and applicable, with fallback to traditional AAA arbitration.

18.9.3. The Company may provide template arbitration language for client series to incorporate into their end-customer agreements.

### 18.10. Survival

18.10.1. This arbitration provision shall survive termination of this Agreement, dissolution of any Series, and termination of any Series under Article 16.

## ARTICLE 19: AI-ENABLED OPERATIONS

19.1. The Company adopts an AI-centric operational philosophy. The Company intends to deploy artificial intelligence, machine learning, and automated systems across its operations to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, regulation, and prudent risk management.

### 19.2. Scope of AI Operations

19.2.1. The Company may use AI-enabled systems for customer identification and verification (KYC/KYB), anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing monitoring (AML/CFT), sanctions screening and watchlist matching, transaction monitoring and suspicious activity detection, risk assessment and scoring, customer support and inquiry handling, document review and contract analysis, regulatory monitoring and compliance research, security testing and vulnerability assessment, incident detection and response, transaction routing and optimization, and any other operational function where AI provides efficiency, accuracy, or cost benefits.

19.2.2. The Company may engage third-party AI service providers, develop proprietary AI systems, or use a combination of both.

### 19.3. Human Oversight Framework

19.3.1. The Company maintains human oversight of AI systems proportionate to the materiality and risk of decisions. The following decisions require human approval regardless of AI recommendations: filing of Suspicious Activity Reports or equivalent regulatory reports, termination of client or end-customer relationships, decisions affecting regulatory licenses or standing, material changes to compliance policies, and disputes involving significant financial exposure.

19.3.2. The Chief Compliance Officer has authority to override AI compliance decisions and to establish thresholds for human review.

19.3.3. The Chief Technology Officer is responsible for AI system performance, monitoring, and maintenance.

19.3.4. As AI technology demonstrates reliability in specific domains, the Manager may authorize expanded autonomous decision-making authority for AI systems, documented in writing with appropriate monitoring and audit procedures.

### 19.4. AI Governance

19.4.1. The Company maintains documentation of AI systems deployed, their purposes, data inputs, decision logic where explainable, performance metrics, and oversight procedures.

19.4.2. AI systems affecting compliance or customer decisions are subject to periodic testing and audit.

19.4.3. The Company monitors regulatory guidance regarding AI use in money services business operations and adapts its AI governance accordingly.

### 19.5. Risk Acknowledgment

19.5.1. Members acknowledge that AI-enabled operations present both opportunities and risks. Opportunities include improved efficiency, reduced costs, enhanced detection capabilities, and scalability. Risks include potential errors, system failures, adversarial attacks, regulatory uncertainty, and technology dependencies.

19.5.2. The Company manages AI-related risks through oversight, testing, fallback procedures, and insurance where available.

### 19.6. Consent to AI Processing

19.6.1. By becoming a Member of any Series, or by using services provided by any Series, parties consent to AI-enabled processing of their information and transactions.

19.6.2. Client series shall inform their end-customers of AI involvement in compliance, support, and operational functions.
