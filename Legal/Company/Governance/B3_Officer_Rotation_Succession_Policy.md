## SECTION 1: PURPOSE

1.1. This Policy establishes procedures for periodic review and rotation of [TERM:Officers], succession planning for [TERM:Officers] positions, emergency succession in case of sudden vacancy, and orderly transitions when [TERM:Officers] depart.

1.2. This Policy ensures continuity of [TERM:Company] operations and compliance with regulatory requirements.

---

## SECTION 2: OFFICER POSITIONS

2.1. The [TERM:Company] has five [TERM:Officers] positions.

2.2. The [TERM:CEO] Chief Executive Officer, abbreviated as CEO, is responsible for overall management, strategy, and regulatory relations.

2.3. The [TERM:CTO] Chief Technology Officer, abbreviated as CTO, is responsible for technology, security, and infrastructure.

2.4. The [TERM:CMO] Chief Marketing Officer, abbreviated as CMO, is responsible for brand, client relations, and business development.

2.5. The [TERM:CFO] Chief Financial Officer, abbreviated as CFO, is responsible for treasury, accounting, and financial reporting.

2.6. The [TERM:CCO] Chief Compliance Officer, abbreviated as CCO, is responsible for the BSA/AML Program, customer identification program, customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, suspicious activity reporting, and regulatory examinations.

## SECTION 3: TERMS AND PERFORMANCE REVIEW
[FLOW:Performance_Review]

### 3.1. Term of Office

3.1.1. [TERM:Officers] serve at the pleasure of the [TERM:Manager]. There is no fixed term.

3.1.2. [TERM:Officers] continue in their positions until resignation, removal by the [TERM:Manager], death or permanent disability, or rotation to another position.

### 3.2. Annual Performance Review
[OBL:OBL-REG-002] <!-- Annual review requirement -->

3.2.1. The [TERM:Manager] shall conduct an annual performance review for each [TERM:Officers] by January 31 of each year, covering the prior calendar year.

3.2.2. The review shall assess goal achievement, meaning whether the [TERM:Officers] met assigned objectives.

3.2.3. The review shall assess operational performance, meaning how effectively the [TERM:Officers] managed their area of responsibility.

3.2.4. The review shall assess compliance, meaning whether the [TERM:Officers] complied with all policies and legal requirements.

3.2.5. The review shall assess leadership, meaning how effectively the [TERM:Officers] led their team.

3.2.6. The review shall assess collaboration, meaning how well the [TERM:Officers] worked with other [TERM:Officers].

3.2.7. The review shall assess growth, meaning whether the [TERM:Officers] has developed professionally.

### 3.3. Performance Rating

3.3.1. Each [TERM:Officers] shall receive a performance rating. An [TERM:Officers] who receives a rating of Exceeds Expectations has demonstrated outstanding performance and is eligible for bonus and promotion consideration.

3.3.2. An [TERM:Officers] who receives a rating of Meets Expectations has demonstrated satisfactory performance and will continue in their position.

3.3.3. An [TERM:Officers] who receives a rating of Needs Improvement has performed below satisfactory levels and a performance improvement plan is required.

3.3.4. An [TERM:Officers] who receives a rating of Unsatisfactory has demonstrated unacceptable performance and is subject to removal.

### 3.4. Performance Improvement Plan

3.4.1. If an [TERM:Officers] receives a Needs Improvement rating, the [TERM:Manager] shall issue a written Performance Improvement Plan.

3.4.2. The Performance Improvement Plan shall specify objectives, timeline (typically ninety days), and support to be provided.

3.4.3. A follow-up review shall occur at the end of the Performance Improvement Plan period.

3.4.4. Failure to improve may result in removal or rotation to another position.

### 3.5. Governance Meetings for Officer Activities

<!-- schema:flow FLOW:Governance_Meetings -->

3.5.1. **Quarterly Officer Meetings.** The [TERM:Manager] shall convene quarterly governance meetings with all [TERM:Officers] to review operational performance, succession plan status, and upcoming rotation or transition matters.

3.5.2. **Meeting Notice.** Quarterly meetings shall be scheduled with at least fourteen (14) days advance notice, specifying date, time, location (or video conference details), and agenda.

3.5.3. **Quorum.** A quorum for Officer governance meetings requires the [TERM:Manager] (or designee) and at least three (3) of the five (5) [TERM:Officers].

3.5.4. **Annual Succession Review Meeting.** An annual succession review meeting shall occur by January 31 of each year, with at least thirty (30) days advance notice, to: (a) review and approve the succession plan for each Officer position; (b) assess development progress of identified successors; (c) review any emergency succession events from the prior year; and (d) approve updates to this Policy.

3.5.5. **Emergency Meetings.** Emergency governance meetings may be called by the [TERM:Manager] or any two [TERM:Officers] with twenty-four (24) hours notice (or less if circumstances require), with quorum of the [TERM:Manager] and two (2) [TERM:Officers].

3.5.6. **Meeting Records.** Minutes of all governance meetings shall be recorded, including attendees, matters discussed, decisions made, and action items. Minutes shall be retained per [DOC:B4].

## SECTION 4: OFFICER ROTATION
[FLOW:Officer_Rotation]

### 4.1. Purpose of Rotation

4.1.1. [TERM:Officers] rotation may be used to develop [TERM:Officers] for broader leadership roles, bring fresh perspective to a functional area, address performance issues without termination, meet regulatory requirements, or serve [TERM:Company] strategic needs.

### 4.2. Rotation Criteria

4.2.1. Before rotating an [TERM:Officers], the [TERM:Manager] shall consider whether the [TERM:Officers] is qualified for the new position.

4.2.2. The [TERM:Manager] shall consider whether there are any licensing or regulatory barriers to the rotation.

4.2.3. The [TERM:Manager] shall consider whether a smooth transition can be achieved for business continuity.

4.2.4. The [TERM:Manager] shall consider whether the [TERM:Officers] agrees to the rotation.

4.2.5. The [TERM:Manager] shall consider whether this is an appropriate time for transition.

### 4.3. Rotation Procedure

4.3.1. The [TERM:Manager] identifies a rotation opportunity.

4.3.2. The [TERM:Manager] discusses the rotation with affected [TERM:Officers].

4.3.3. The [TERM:Manager] issues written rotation notice thirty days before the rotation effective date.

4.3.4. Transition planning begins during the thirty-day notice period.

4.3.5. Knowledge transfer occurs during the notice period.

4.3.6. The rotation becomes effective on the date stated in the notice.

4.3.7. Post-rotation support continues for thirty days after rotation.

### 4.4. Rotation Notice

4.4.1. A rotation notice shall include the current position and new position, the effective date of rotation, transition requirements, any compensation changes, and reporting relationships in the new position.

### 4.5. Refusal to Rotate

4.5.1. If an [TERM:Officers] refuses a rotation, the [TERM:Manager] may accept the refusal and explore alternatives.

4.5.2. The [TERM:Manager] may require the rotation as a condition of continued employment.

4.5.3. The [TERM:Officers] may choose to resign.

4.5.4. The [TERM:Manager] may remove the [TERM:Officers].

## SECTION 5: SUCCESSION PLANNING
[FLOW:Succession_Planning]

### 5.1. Succession Plan Requirement

5.1.1. The [TERM:Manager] shall maintain a written succession plan that identifies at least one potential successor for each [TERM:Officers] position.

### 5.2. Succession Plan Contents

5.2.1. For each [TERM:Officers] position, the plan shall identify a primary successor, being the first choice to fill the position.

5.2.2. The plan shall identify a secondary successor, being a backup if the primary is unavailable.

5.2.3. The plan shall identify external candidate criteria, being the qualifications required for an outside hire if needed.

5.2.4. The plan shall identify development needs, being the training or experience the successors need.

5.2.5. The plan shall identify the timeline, being how quickly each successor could assume the role.

### 5.3. Succession Plan Review

5.3.1. The [TERM:Manager] shall review and update the succession plan at least annually.
[OBL:OBL-REG-002]

5.3.2. The [TERM:Manager] shall review and update the succession plan whenever an [TERM:Officers] departs.

5.3.3. The [TERM:Manager] shall review and update the succession plan whenever a potential successor's status changes.

5.3.4. The [TERM:Manager] shall review and update the succession plan whenever [TERM:Company] structure significantly changes.

### 5.4. Successor Development

5.4.1. The [TERM:Manager] shall ensure potential successors receive cross-training in their target position.

5.4.2. Potential successors shall receive mentoring from current [TERM:Officers].

5.4.3. Potential successors shall receive exposure to board and regulatory interactions.

5.4.4. Potential successors shall receive leadership development opportunities.

## SECTION 6: EMERGENCY SUCCESSION
[FLOW:Emergency_Succession]

### 6.1. Emergency Defined

6.1.1. An emergency succession situation exists when an [TERM:Officers] is suddenly unable to serve due to death, serious illness or disability, immediate termination for cause, resignation without adequate notice, or incapacity.

### 6.2. Immediate Actions

6.2.1. Within twenty-four hours of an emergency vacancy, the first [TERM:Officers] aware of the situation shall notify the [TERM:Manager].

6.2.2. The [TERM:CTO], or their backup, shall secure [TERM:Company] assets including IT systems and access credentials.

6.2.3. The [TERM:CEO], or their backup, shall notify remaining [TERM:Officers].

6.2.4. The [TERM:Manager] shall appoint an interim [TERM:Officers].

6.2.5. The [TERM:CEO], or their backup, shall notify key stakeholders.

### 6.3. Interim Officer Appointment

6.3.1. The [TERM:Manager] may immediately appoint an interim [TERM:Officers] from the succession plan primary successor, another current [TERM:Officers] in a dual role, the [TERM:Manager] personally, or an external interim executive.

### 6.4. Permanent Replacement Timeline

6.4.1. An interim [TERM:Officers] shall be appointed within twenty-four hours.

6.4.2. A permanent search shall begin within seven days.

6.4.3. The search shall be completed within forty-five days.

6.4.4. A permanent [TERM:Officers] shall be appointed within sixty days.

### 6.5. Default Succession

6.5.1. If no interim appointment is made, the following default succession applies. If the [TERM:CEO] position is vacant, the [TERM:Manager] assumes the role. If the [TERM:CTO] position is vacant, the [TERM:CEO] assumes the role, or if the [TERM:CEO] is unavailable, the [TERM:Manager] assumes the role. If the [TERM:CMO] position is vacant, the [TERM:CEO] assumes the role, or if the [TERM:CEO] is unavailable, the [TERM:Manager] assumes the role. If the [TERM:CFO] position is vacant, the [TERM:CEO] assumes the role, or if the [TERM:CEO] is unavailable, the [TERM:Manager] assumes the role. If the [TERM:CCO] position is vacant, the [TERM:CEO] assumes the BSA/AML Officer role on an interim basis. An interim BSA/AML Officer with appropriate qualifications shall be designated within seventy-two hours. FinCEN shall be notified of any BSA Officer change as required by applicable regulations.
[OBL:OBL-REG-001] <!-- FinCEN notification requirement -->

### 6.5A. Multiple Simultaneous Vacancies

<!-- schema:flow FLOW:Multiple_Vacancy_Response -->

6.5A.1. **Multiple Officer Vacancies.** If two or more [TERM:Officers] positions become vacant simultaneously (e.g., CEO and CTO, or multiple Officers due to shared incident):
   (a) The [TERM:Manager] shall immediately assume or delegate all essential management functions;
   (b) The [TERM:Manager] may appoint a qualified external interim executive or management consultant within forty-eight (48) hours;
   (c) Remaining [TERM:Officers] may temporarily assume dual roles only where no conflict of interest exists (e.g., CCO may not assume CFO if financial controls are relevant to compliance investigation);
   (d) Critical regulatory functions (BSA/AML Officer, custody controls) shall be assigned to qualified individuals within seventy-two (72) hours per §6.6.

6.5A.2. **Catastrophic Vacancy (3+ Officers).** If three or more [TERM:Officers] positions become vacant simultaneously:
   (a) The [TERM:Manager] shall invoke the Business Continuity Plan per [DOC:B6] §7;
   (b) An emergency management committee of the [TERM:Manager] plus any remaining [TERM:Officers] shall be convened within twenty-four (24) hours;
   (c) External interim management engagement is authorized without standard approval thresholds;
   (d) Banking partners, key vendors, and regulators shall be notified within five (5) business days if operations are materially affected;
   (e) The [TERM:Manager] shall report the situation and response plan to Members within ten (10) business days.

6.5A.3. **Deputy and Backup Designations.** To mitigate multiple vacancy risk:
   (a) Each [TERM:Officers] shall identify a qualified deputy or backup (internal or external) in their succession plan;
   (b) Deputy designations shall be reviewed quarterly;
   (c) Deputies shall have documented access to critical systems, runbooks, and key ceremony procedures per [DOC:B6] §6.1.

## SECTION 6.6: BSA/AML OFFICER SUCCESSION REQUIREMENTS
[FLOW:BSA_Officer_Succession]

6.6.1. Due to the regulatory significance of the BSA/AML Officer role, the following additional requirements apply to [TERM:CCO] succession:

6.6.2. An interim BSA/AML Officer must be designated within seventy-two hours of any [TERM:CCO] vacancy.

6.6.3. The interim BSA/AML Officer must have sufficient knowledge of BSA/AML requirements to fulfill the role's essential functions.

6.6.4. **FinCEN Form 107 Update Clarification.** FinCEN Form 107 must be updated within the timeframes required by 31 CFR §1022.380(b)(3) only if the departing or incoming BSA/AML Officer is also a principal owner, responsible person, or control person of the MSB. Note: 31 CFR §1022.380 governs MSB registration updates for owners and control persons; BSA/AML Officer designation changes under the AML program requirement (31 CFR §1022.210) do not independently trigger Form 107 updates unless the officer also holds a control person role. The Company shall track BSA/AML Officer changes internally per [DOC:B4] regardless of Form 107 requirements.
[DOC:B5] <!-- BSA/AML Program - FinCEN reporting requirements -->

6.6.5. State money transmitter license authorities must be notified of [TERM:CCO] changes within the timeframes specified in each applicable license.

6.6.6. A permanent [TERM:CCO] replacement must meet the qualification standards set forth in the [DOC:B9] Fit and Proper Screening Policy and shall be appointed within sixty days.

## SECTION 7: VOLUNTARY DEPARTURE
[FLOW:Voluntary_Departure]

### 7.1. Resignation Notice

7.1.1. An [TERM:Officers] who wishes to resign shall provide written notice to the [TERM:Manager], at least thirty days advance notice (sixty days preferred), and may optionally provide a reason for departure.

### 7.2. Manager Response

7.2.1. Upon receiving resignation notice, the [TERM:Manager] may accept the resignation effective as stated.

7.2.2. The [TERM:Manager] may request an earlier effective date.

7.2.3. The [TERM:Manager] may request an extension of the notice period.

7.2.4. The [TERM:Manager] may negotiate transition terms.

### 7.3. Transition Requirements

7.3.1. A departing [TERM:Officers] shall prepare a written transition memo covering current projects and status, key relationships and contacts, pending decisions and issues, and recommendations for successor.

7.3.2. A departing [TERM:Officers] shall transfer all [TERM:Company] property including computers and devices, keys and access cards, documents and files, and passwords and credentials.

7.3.3. A departing [TERM:Officers] shall complete an exit interview with the [TERM:Manager].

7.3.4. A departing [TERM:Officers] shall sign required separation documents.

### 7.4. Cooperation Period

7.4.1. A departing [TERM:Officers] shall remain available for questions for thirty days after departure.

7.4.2. This cooperation period may be included in the notice period, a separate consulting arrangement, or an informal professional courtesy.

8.1.1. The [TERM:Manager] may remove any [TERM:Officers] at any time, with or without cause.

### 8.2. Removal for Cause

8.2.1. Cause for immediate removal includes fraud, embezzlement, or theft.

8.2.2. Cause for immediate removal includes conviction of a felony.

8.2.3. Cause for immediate removal includes willful misconduct or gross negligence.

8.2.4. Cause for immediate removal includes breach of fiduciary duty.

8.2.5. Cause for immediate removal includes violation of [TERM:Company] policies.

8.2.6. Cause for immediate removal includes regulatory disqualification.

8.2.7. Cause for immediate removal includes refusal to perform duties.

8.2.8. Cause for immediate removal includes actions harmful to the [TERM:Company].

### 8.3. Removal Without Cause

8.3.1. The [TERM:Manager] may remove an [TERM:Officers] without cause for strategic or organizational reasons.

8.3.2. The [TERM:Manager] may remove an [TERM:Officers] without cause due to performance concerns.

8.3.3. The [TERM:Manager] may remove an [TERM:Officers] without cause due to budget constraints.

8.3.4. The [TERM:Manager] may remove an [TERM:Officers] without cause for any reason or no reason.

### 8.4. Removal Procedure

8.4.1. The [TERM:Manager] documents reasons for removal.

8.4.2. The [TERM:Manager] consults legal counsel if appropriate.

8.4.3. The [TERM:Manager] meets with the [TERM:Officers] to communicate the decision.

8.4.4. The [TERM:Manager] collects [TERM:Company] property.

8.4.5. The [TERM:Manager] terminates access to IT systems, facilities, and accounts.

## SECTION 10: CONFIDENTIALITY AND NON-COMPETITION

### 10.1. Confidentiality Obligations

10.1.1. All [TERM:Officers] have ongoing confidentiality obligations that survive departure. These obligations cover trade secrets and proprietary information, client information and relationships, financial information, strategic plans, and technology and systems information.

### 10.2. Non-Competition

10.2.1. **Montana Law Limitation.** Pursuant to MCA §28-2-703 and §28-2-704, non-competition agreements are generally void and unenforceable under Montana law, except in limited circumstances involving the sale of business goodwill. The [TERM:Company] does not require or enforce post-employment non-competition covenants for [TERM:Officers].

10.2.2. **Permitted Protections.** The [TERM:Company] relies on the following lawful protections in lieu of non-compete agreements:
(a) Confidentiality obligations as set forth in Section 10.1, which survive departure indefinitely for trade secrets and for a reasonable period for other confidential information;
(b) Non-solicitation covenants as set forth in Section 10.3, which are enforceable under Montana law;
(c) Intellectual property assignment agreements;
(d) Fiduciary duties during the term of service.

10.2.3. **Exception for Business Sale.** Non-competition covenants may be included in connection with the sale or transfer of the goodwill of a business pursuant to MCA §28-2-703, if applicable to a departing [TERM:Officers] who holds an ownership interest being sold.

### 10.3. Non-Solicitation

10.3.1. [TERM:Officers] shall not, for twenty-four months after departure, solicit [TERM:Company] employees.

10.3.2. [TERM:Officers] shall not, for twenty-four months after departure, solicit [TERM:Company] clients.

10.3.3. [TERM:Officers] shall not, for twenty-four months after departure, interfere with [TERM:Company] business relationships.
