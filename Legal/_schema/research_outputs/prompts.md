## RO-005: Sponsor Bank & Banking Partner Models

```
You are a regulatory research paralegal specializing in banking law and MSB compliance. Conduct exhaustive research on sponsor bank relationships and banking access for Money Services Businesses.

RESEARCH SCOPE:

1. CURRENT BANKING LANDSCAPE FOR MSBs
   - Which U.S. banks actively provide banking services to licensed MSBs? (Name specific institutions where publicly known)
   - What are the typical account types offered (operating accounts, settlement accounts, custodial accounts)?
   - What due diligence requirements do banks impose on MSB clients?
   - Fee structures and minimum balance requirements typical for MSB banking relationships
   - Geographic limitations or state-specific banking availability

2. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK FOR BANKS SERVING MSBs
   - Bank Secrecy Act obligations banks assume when banking MSBs (31 USC 5318, 31 CFR 1020)
   - OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve guidance on banks providing services to MSBs
   - The 2005 Joint Statement on providing banking services to MSBs
   - The 2014 FDIC clarification on MSB account termination (de-risking concerns)
   - FinCEN guidance on banks' obligations regarding MSB customers
   - Recent enforcement actions against banks for MSB-related BSA violations

3. DE-RISKING AND ACCOUNT TERMINATION
   - Document the de-risking trend affecting MSBs (2010-present)
   - Regulatory responses to MSB de-banking (Operation Choke Point history and aftermath)
   - Current state of MSB access to banking (has it improved since 2017?)
   - Best practices for MSBs to avoid account termination
   - Red flags that trigger bank account closures for MSBs
   - Legal recourse available to MSBs facing de-banking

4. BANKING-AS-A-SERVICE (BaaS) AND ALTERNATIVE MODELS
   - BaaS providers that serve MSBs (identify specific platforms)
   - How BaaS relationships are structured (sponsor bank + middleware + MSB)
   - Compliance responsibilities in the BaaS model
   - Regulatory treatment of BaaS arrangements for MSB clients
   - Recent OCC and FDIC guidance on bank-fintech partnerships affecting MSBs

5. SPONSOR BANK MODEL SPECIFICS
   - What is a "sponsor bank" in the MSB context vs. payment processor context?
   - Contractual requirements typically imposed by sponsor banks
   - Compliance program requirements sponsors impose on MSBs
   - Audit and examination rights retained by sponsor banks
   - Liability allocation between sponsor bank and MSB
   - Termination provisions and wind-down procedures

6. COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS BANKS IMPOSE
   - Typical MSB compliance program elements required by banking partners
   - Third-party audit requirements
   - Insurance and bonding requirements beyond state minimums
   - Transaction monitoring and reporting obligations
   - Customer due diligence standards banks expect MSBs to maintain

DELIVERABLES:
- Comprehensive memorandum on MSB banking landscape with citations
- Bank/BaaS provider comparison matrix (where publicly available)
- Compliance checklist: what banks require from MSB clients
- Risk mitigation strategies for maintaining banking relationships
- Template due diligence package outline for approaching potential banking partners

Prioritize primary sources: regulatory guidance, examination manuals, enforcement orders, public statements. Include industry reports from credible sources (CSBS, MBA, etc.) where helpful.
```

## RO-006: Agent-of-Payee Exemption Deep Dive

```
You are a regulatory research paralegal specializing in money transmission law. Conduct exhaustive research on the agent-of-payee exemption from money transmitter licensing requirements.

RESEARCH SCOPE:

1. FEDERAL TREATMENT
   - FinCEN's position on agent-of-payee arrangements (cite specific guidance)
   - 2011 Final Rule on MSB definitions and how it addresses payment processors
   - FinCEN administrative rulings on agent-of-payee scenarios
   - How agent-of-payee relates to the "payment processor" exemption under 31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5)(ii)(A)-(F)
   - Recent FinCEN enforcement actions involving agent-of-payee claims

2. STATE-BY-STATE ANALYSIS
   - Create comprehensive chart of all 50 states + DC position on agent-of-payee
   - Categories: (a) Statutory exemption exists, (b) Regulatory guidance recognizes, (c) Silent but likely recognized, (d) Expressly rejected, (e) Unclear
   - For each state with an exemption, document the specific requirements to qualify
   - States with recent legislative or regulatory changes affecting this exemption
   - Focus states: MT, CA, NY, TX, FL, IL, WA, GA, PA, OH (high-volume states)

3. QUALIFYING REQUIREMENTS (COMMON ELEMENTS)
   - Written agency agreement requirements (what must it contain?)
   - Scope limitations (what activities can/cannot be performed?)
   - Disclosure requirements to end consumers
   - Who can be the "payee" (merchant vs. other entities)?
   - Settlement timing requirements (immediate vs. delayed)
   - Handling of customer funds (segregation, commingling rules)
   - Recordkeeping obligations even when exempt from licensing

4. PRACTICAL APPLICATION SCENARIOS
   - Payment facilitators (PayFac model) and agent-of-payee
   - Marketplace platforms (how Airbnb, Uber, etc. structure for exemption)
   - SaaS platforms collecting subscription payments on behalf of vendors
   - Invoice factoring and accounts receivable arrangements
   - Crowdfunding platforms
   - Property management collecting rent on behalf of landlords

5. ENFORCEMENT AND CASE LAW
   - State enforcement actions where agent-of-payee was claimed but rejected
   - Court cases interpreting agent-of-payee requirements
   - Consent orders or settlements involving failed agent-of-payee claims
   - Warning signs that an agent-of-payee structure will not be respected

6. STRUCTURING BEST PRACTICES
   - Essential contract provisions for valid agent-of-payee arrangements
   - Operational requirements to maintain exemption validity
   - Documentation and evidence preservation recommendations
   - When to seek a formal regulatory opinion vs. rely on self-assessment
   - Hybrid structures: what if some transactions qualify and others don't?

7. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER EXEMPTIONS
   - How agent-of-payee interacts with bank exemption
   - Comparison to authorized delegate/agent model (licensed MTL using agents)
   - Payment processor exemption vs. agent-of-payee (different or overlapping?)
   - Third-party sender (TPS) model under NACHA rules vs. agent-of-payee

DELIVERABLES:
- Comprehensive legal memorandum with full citations
- 50-state + DC comparison chart with exemption status and requirements
- Decision tree: "Does your business model qualify for agent-of-payee?"
- Sample contract provisions checklist for agent-of-payee agreements
- Risk assessment: factors that strengthen or weaken exemption claims

Prioritize primary sources: state statutes, regulations, official guidance, administrative rulings, enforcement orders, case law. Note jurisdictions where the law is ambiguous or silent.
```
