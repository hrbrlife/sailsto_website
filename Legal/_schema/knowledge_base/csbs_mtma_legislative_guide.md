Background
•   The Money Transmission Modernization Act (“MTMA”) is a set of nationwide standards and
    requirements for money transmission designed to protect consumers and enable local innovation.
•   The MTMA:
        o establishes a common baseline to be used across the country;
        o advances harmonization in the money transmission industry, making it easier for
           companies to operate across state lines;
        o enables states to better work together in the licensing, regulation and supervision of
           money transmitters;
        o strengthens consumer protection through enhanced prudential standards and disclosure
           requirements.

The MTMA makes money transmission regulation more efficient and effective for a growing industry.

The MTMA is a collaborative response to widespread criticism of state regulation of money
transmitters.
•   Criticism of state regulation in the money transmission industry has been paramount for the past
    decade with continued calls for federal preemption.
•   To identify the pain points driving criticisms, CSBS formed the Fintech Industry Advisory Panel in
    2018, which made specific recommendations, including the development of model legislation for
    money transmitters.
•   Because the pain points were rooted in variation between state laws, CSBS committed to drafting a
    model law.
•   To ensure such a model law would be collaborative, CSBS Board established the Regulator-Industry
    Clearing House, a joint working group of state regulators and industry experts, to draft statutory
    language that meets the policy objectives set by the CSBS Board.

The MTMA improves operating conditions for companies, supervisory capabilities for regulators, and
protections for consumers.

    point for industry and a key talking point for proponents of preemption
