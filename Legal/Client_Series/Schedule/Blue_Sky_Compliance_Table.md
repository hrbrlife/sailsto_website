## FEDERAL PREEMPTION SUMMARY

**National Securities Markets Improvement Act (NSMIA) § 18(b)(4)(D):**

**Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) offerings are "covered securities":**
- ✓ States **CANNOT** require registration or merit review
- ✓ States **CAN** require notice filing + filing fee
- ✓ States **CAN** require consent to service of process
- ✓ States **CAN** enforce anti-fraud provisions
- ✓ States **CAN** bring enforcement actions for violations

**Rule 504 offerings are NOT covered securities:**
- States MAY require full registration (merit review)
- Compliance varies significantly by state

**This table focuses on Rule 506(b)/506(c) offerings used by Sails Series.**

## CONSENT TO SERVICE OF PROCESS

**What It Is:**

A legal document designating the state securities administrator as the Issuer's agent to receive service of legal process (lawsuits, subpoenas, etc.) in that state.

**Why Required:**

States require Consent so they can enforce securities laws against out-of-state issuers. If state needs to sue or investigate Issuer, state serves documents on state securities administrator, who forwards to Issuer.

**Format:**

Most states provide standard Consent form on securities division website. Typical format:

```
CONSENT TO SERVICE OF PROCESS

The undersigned Issuer, [Sails DAO LLC - Issuer Series I-XXX], hereby irrevocably 
consents that service of process may be made upon it in any civil action or proceeding 
brought in [STATE NAME] against Issuer or Issuer's successors arising out of or in 
connection with the offer or sale of securities, by serving the [STATE] Securities 
Commissioner or other designated agent with a copy of such process.

Issuer: Sails DAO LLC - Issuer Series I-XXX
Address: [Address]
Signature: __________________________
Name: [Series Manager Name]
Title: Series Manager
Date: [DATE]
```

**Notarization:**

Some states require Consent to be notarized. Check state-specific requirements.

**Irrevocability:**

Consent is irrevocable. Once filed, cannot be withdrawn even after offering concludes. Issuer remains subject to state jurisdiction for claims arising from offering indefinitely (subject to statute of limitations).

- Federal Form D: $0
- 10 State filings @ avg $275: **$2,750**
- Annual renewals (if offering >1 year): **$2,750/year**
- **Total Year 1:** $2,750

**Scenario 2: 25 States (Larger Offering)**
- Federal Form D: $0
- 25 State filings @ avg $275: **$6,875**
- Annual renewals: **$6,875/year**
- **Total Year 1:** $6,875

**Scenario 3: 50 States (National Offering)**
- Federal Form D: $0
- 47 State filings (excluding GA, NC, VA which require no filing) @ avg $275: **$12,925**
- Annual renewals: **$12,925/year**
- **Total Year 1:** $12,925

**Cost Comparison to Broker-Dealer Registration:**
- Broker-dealer FINRA registration: $100,000-$300,000 initial + $200,000-$500,000/year ongoing
- Blue Sky notice filing: $3,000-$13,000 initial + $3,000-$13,000/year ongoing
- **Savings:** $97,000-$487,000/year by using agent-of-payee model vs broker-dealer

## COMMON PITFALLS AND BEST PRACTICES

**PITFALLS TO AVOID:**

1. **Missing 15-Day Deadline**
   - Calculate deadline from **first sale** (when subscription accepted and payment received), NOT from offering commencement
   - Calendar reminder: Set reminder for Day 10 after first sale to ensure filing by Day 15
   
2. **Filing in Wrong States**
   - File in EVERY state where investors reside, not just where offering marketed
   - If investor moves after subscription, file in new state
   
3. **Forgetting Colorado Pre-Filing**
   - Colorado requires filing BEFORE sale; cannot sell to CO residents until filing accepted
   - Other states allow 15 days post-sale
   
4. **Using Wrong Form for New York**
   - NY requires Form 99 (state form), NOT federal Form D
   
5. **Missing Annual Renewals**
   - If offering continues >1 year, annual renewal required in most states
   - Deadline: 30 days before anniversary (varies by state)
   
6. **Not Filing Termination Notice**
   - When offering concludes, file amended Form D with SEC and states marking "Terminated"
   - Failure to file termination = continued annual renewal obligations
   
7. **Listing Payment Processor as Sales Compensation**
   - Agent-of-payee payment processors are NOT "sales compensation recipients"
   - Processor receives payment processing fees, not sales commissions
   - Do NOT list processor in Item 12 of Form D
   
8. **PPM Inconsistencies with Form D**
   - Ensure offering amount, use of proceeds, investor minimums in PPM match Form D disclosures
   - Discrepancies trigger scrutiny

**BEST PRACTICES:**

1. **File Proactively**
   - File federal Form D on Day 1 after first sale (don't wait until Day 15)
   - File state Form Ds concurrently with federal filing
   
2. **Use Checklist**
   - Maintain checklist (see Section VI of Form D Filing Guide) for each offering
   - Track filing dates, confirmation numbers, renewal deadlines
   
3. **Centralized Recordkeeping**
   - Store all Form D filings (federal and state) in Company records [DOC:B1 §11]
   - 5-year retention required
   
4. **Quarterly Compliance Review**
   - Review Form D filings quarterly for material changes requiring amendment
   - Include in quarterly compliance review [DOC:B1 §8.9]
   
5. **State Fee Budget**
   - Budget $3,000-$13,000 for state filing fees depending on investor geographic spread
   - Budget annual renewal costs if offering >1 year
   
6. **Counsel Review**
   - Have securities counsel review first Form D before filing
   - Complex or large offerings (>$10M, >25 states) may justify filing service (CSC, Cogency, NSC)
   
7. **Investor Screening**
   - Ask investors for state of residence BEFORE accepting subscription
   - If state has complex requirements or high fees, consider investor suitability
   - Colorado pre-filing: Inform CO investors of 1-2 day delay for filing acceptance
   
8. **Subscription Agreement Clause**
   - Include clause in Subscription Agreement: "Issuer will file Form D with SEC and applicable state securities authorities. Investor consents to disclosure of information on Form D."
