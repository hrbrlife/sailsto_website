## I. OVERVIEW

**A. What is Form D?**

Form D is a brief notice filing required under Regulation D of the Securities Act of 1933. It is NOT a registration statement. It is an informational notice to the SEC and state securities authorities that a company has sold securities under a Regulation D exemption from registration.

**B. When is Form D Required?**

Form D required when Series conducts securities offering under **Regulation D, Rules 504, 505, or 506**. 

**Filing Deadline:** Within **15 calendar days** after the **first sale** of securities in the offering.

**"First Sale" Definition:** First sale occurs when investor is irrevocably obligated to purchase securities AND Series is irrevocably obligated to issue securities. Typically: when Series signs Subscription Agreement accepting investor's subscription and receives payment (or binding commitment to pay).

**C. Where is Form D Filed?**

1. **Federal:** Electronic filing with SEC via EDGAR system
2. **State:** Filing with state securities regulators in states where:
   - Issuer is domiciled (Wyoming for Series)
   - Investors reside
   - Sales are made (if different from investor residence)

**D. Filing Fees**

- **SEC:** No fee for federal Form D filing
- **State:** Fees vary by state (typically $0-$500 per state); see Section V

**E. Public Disclosure**

Form D is **publicly available** information. Once filed with SEC, anyone can view Form D on SEC EDGAR database. Information disclosed includes:
- Issuer name, address, and identity
- Names of executive officers, directors, promoters
- Amount of offering
- Number of investors
- Use of proceeds (general categories)
- Names and addresses of persons paid sales commissions (if any)

**F. Consequences of Non-Filing or Late Filing**

- **Federal:** SEC has authority to impose penalties including:
  - Cease-and-desist orders
  - Civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation per day
  - Loss of ability to rely on Regulation D exemption in future (Bad Actor disqualification under Rule 506(d))
  
- **State:** State securities regulators may:
  - Assess fines
  - Issue stop orders prohibiting further sales
  - Require rescission offers to investors (return of investment + interest - damages)
  - Bring enforcement actions

**CRITICAL:** Form D filing is ministerial act. Failure to file cannot be excused by ignorance. Series Manager responsible for ensuring timely filing.

## II. FORM D FEDERAL FILING - EDGAR PROCEDURES

**A. Obtain EDGAR Access Codes**

Before filing Form D, Series must obtain EDGAR access credentials from SEC.

**Step 1: Generate EDGAR Filer Access Codes**

1. Go to: https://www.filermanagement.edgarfiling.sec.gov/
2. Click "**New User – Request Access Codes**"
3. Select "**Company**" (not individual)
4. Complete Form ID application:
   - Legal name: Sails DAO LLC - Issuer Series I-[XXX]
   - CIK (Central Index Key): Leave blank for new filer; SEC will assign
   - Jurisdiction: Wyoming
   - Business address: [Registered agent address]
   - Mailing address: [Operating address]
   - Contact information: Series Manager or authorized officer
   - Signature: Typed electronic signature of Series Manager
5. Submit application
6. SEC will **mail** EDGAR access codes to address on file within **1-2 business days**
   - **CIK** (Central Index Key): Unique identifier for Series
   - **CCC** (CIK Confirmation Code): Password for EDGAR system
   - **PMAC** (Password Modification Authorization Code): Allows password changes

**IMPORTANT:** Keep EDGAR codes confidential. Anyone with CIK and CCC can file documents on behalf of Series.

**Step 2: Complete EDGAR Filer Manual Training (Optional but Recommended)**

SEC provides free online training: https://www.sec.gov/edgar/filer-information

Review:
- EDGAR Filer Manual Volume II (for Form D)
- EDGARLink Online tutorial

**D. Form D Amendments**

Form D must be **amended** when material information changes:

**Required Amendments (File "Amendment" Form D):**
- Change in address or contact information
- Change in executive officers, directors, or promoters
- Change in federal exemption claimed (e.g., switching from 506(b) to 506(c))
- Additional sales beyond maximum offering amount disclosed
- Termination of offering

**Annual Amendment (if offering continues >1 year):**
- File updated Form D within **30 days** of each anniversary of first filing
- Update offering and sales amounts, investor count
- Even if no sales in past year, amendment required to keep offering active

**Timing:** Amendments generally due within **30 days** of change or annually.

**Procedure:** Same as initial filing but select "Amendment" in Item 7.

## III. STATE FORM D FILINGS ("BLUE SKY" COMPLIANCE)

**A. Federal Preemption Under NSMIA**

National Securities Markets Improvement Act (NSMIA) of 1996 provides **federal preemption** for certain offerings, limiting state authority:

**Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c):** "Covered Securities" under NSMIA § 18(b)(4)(D)
- States **cannot require registration or qualification** (merit review)
- States **CAN require** notice filing + fee
- States **CAN** enforce anti-fraud laws
- States **CAN** require exiting investors (rescission, restitution)

**Rule 504:** NOT covered securities
- States **CAN require full registration** (merit review)
- Varies by state; some states allow notice filing, others require full review

**B. State Filing Requirements Summary**

For **Rule 506(b) or 506(c) offerings** (the vast majority of Sails Series offerings):

**1. Notice Filing States (Majority)**

Most states allow **notice filing** for Rule 506 offerings:
- File Form D (state version or copy of federal Form D)
- Pay filing fee ($100-$500 typically)
- Include copy of PPM (some states)
- Include consent to service of process (some states)
- Deadline: **15 days after first sale** (aligns with federal deadline)

**2. No Filing Requirement States**

A few states require **no filing** for Rule 506 offerings:
- No Form D required
- No fee
- Simply comply with anti-fraud rules

**3. Pre-Filing States**

A few states require **notice filing BEFORE first sale** to residents of that state:
- Cannot sell to state residents until notice filed and accepted
- Filing typically processed within 1-5 business days

**D. State Filing Procedures**

**Step 1: Identify States**

List all states where:
- Investors reside (based on Subscription Agreements)
- Offering was marketed (if Rule 506(c))
- Series has physical presence (Wyoming)

**Step 2: Determine Requirements**

For each state, look up:
- Notice filing requirement (yes/no)
- Pre-filing vs. post-first-sale
- Filing fee
- Required forms and documents

**Step 3: Prepare State Filing Package**

Typical package includes:
1. **Federal Form D** (copy from EDGAR, OR state-specific form if required)
2. **Consent to Service of Process** (state form)
   - Designates state securities administrator as agent for service of legal process
   - Typically requires notarization
3. **Filing Fee** (check or credit card)
4. **Copy of PPM** (some states require)
5. **Copy of Subscription Agreement** (some states require)
6. **List of State Residents Who Invested** (some states require)

**Step 4: File with State**

**Filing Methods (vary by state):**

**A. Electronic Filing via CRD (Central Registration Depository):**
- Some states use FINRA's CRD system for Form D notice filings
- Requires CRD account (typically used by broker-dealers; may be available for issuers in some states)
- Portal: https://www.finra.org/registration-exams-ce/crd

**B. Electronic Filing via State Portal:**
- Some states have proprietary electronic filing systems
- Examples: California CFRIS, Florida SIPS
- Create account on state portal and submit documents electronically

**C. Mail or Email:**
- Some states accept filings by mail or email
- Mail certified/return receipt recommended
- Email to state securities division (confirm email address on state website)

**Addresses:**
- Find state securities regulator contact at: https://www.nasaa.org/contact-your-regulator/
- Typical format: "[State] Securities Division", "[State] Department of Financial Institutions", etc.

**Step 5: Track Filings**

Maintain spreadsheet tracking:
- State
- Filing date
- Filing method
- Confirmation number (if electronic)
- Fee paid
- Status (filed, accepted, rejected)
- Renewal date (if annual renewal required)

**E. State Annual Renewals**

Some states require **annual renewal** of notice filing if offering continues >1 year:
- File renewal notice (typically federal Form D amendment)
- Pay renewal fee (typically same as initial fee)
- Deadline: **30 days before anniversary** of initial filing (varies by state)

**F. State Termination Notices**

When offering concludes:
- File **Form D Amendment** with SEC marking offering as "Terminated"
- File **termination notices** with states (typically federal Form D amendment)
- Deadline: **30 days after termination** (varies by state)

**G. Penalties for Non-Compliance**

States may impose:
- Fines ($1,000-$10,000 per violation)
- Stop orders (prohibiting further sales in state)
- Rescission liability (investors can demand return of investment + interest)
- Criminal penalties (for willful violations)

## IV. ONGOING FORM D COMPLIANCE

**A. Annual Amendments (Federal and State)**

If offering continues beyond 1 year from first sale:
- File **amended Form D** with SEC within **30 days of anniversary**
- File **amended Form D** with states (where required) within **30 days of anniversary**
- Update offering and sales amounts, investor count, executive officers/directors

**Even if no sales occurred during year, amendment required to keep offering "active".**

**B. Material Change Amendments**

File amended Form D within **30 days** of material changes:
- Change in offering amount
- Change in securities type
- Change in federal exemption claimed
- Change in executive officers or directors
- Change in address or contact information
- Change in related persons (promoters, etc.)

**C. Termination of Offering**

When offering concludes (maximum amount raised OR offering period expires OR Series elects to terminate):
- File **final amended Form D** with SEC within **30 days**
- Mark offering as "Terminated"
- Update final total amounts sold and investor count
- File **termination notice** with states within **30 days**

**D. Record Retention**

Maintain records for **5 years** after termination of offering:
- Copy of Form D as filed (federal and state)
- EDGAR confirmation and accession number
- State filing confirmations
- Evidence of payment of fees
- Evidence of timely filing (postmark, electronic timestamp)
