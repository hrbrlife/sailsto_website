⛵ Sails.to - How It All Works

What is Sails.to?

Sails.to is a platform that lets companies raise money by selling digital investments (called "security tokens") to professional investors. Think of it as a system that makes it possible to buy and sell pieces of loans or business projects — but everything is tracked on a blockchain (Solana) so it's transparent and secure.

The key difference from regular crypto: These aren't memecoins. They're real investments with legal rules about who can buy them and how they can be traded.

2. How New Investments Are Created

When a company wants to raise money through Sails.to, the platform provides them with a "Series" — a dedicated legal entity (SPV) with defined investment terms, ready for tokenization.

The Structure: Series LLC

Sails.to structures each investment as a "Series" under a Series LLC — either a Wyoming Series LLC or a Marshall Islands DAO LLC, depending on the issuer's needs. Each Series is a fully isolated legal container: its own assets, liabilities, and investor registry are completely walled off from every other Series. This encapsulation ensures clean separation between issuances.

The Fundraising System

Every offering has two important numbers and two pricing phases:

Term What It Means
Soft Cap The minimum amount that must be raised for the deal to happen
Hard Cap The maximum amount that can be raised
Duration Maximum time the offering stays open (default: 4 months)

Early Bird (Optional Discount)

From opening until the soft cap is reached, the issuer can offer an optional discount to early investors. This incentivizes people to commit early and helps reach the soft cap faster.

Full Price

Once the soft cap is reached, the discount ends. Additional investments up to the hard cap are at full price ($150,000 per security) until the offering ends.

When Does the Offering End?

The offering closes when either of these happens first:

  • Hard cap is met — maximum funding reached, offering closes immediately
  • Duration expires — 4 months (default) pass, offering closes at current level

Once soft cap is reached, the project is live and funds flow to the issuer. Additional investors can still join until the offering ends.

How Channel 3 Works

Some investors only work with traditional securities infrastructure. Channel 3 bridges that gap:

  1. Issuer locks tokens in the Lockbox Custody contract
  2. Tokens are wrapped 1:1 into ISIN-bearing securities
  3. The issuer's paying agent bank distributes them via Clearstream

The same investment, accessible through traditional finance rails.

Fees

Fees depend on how the investor found the offering — tracked via referral codes. Investors brought in by the issuer's own network cost less than those sourced through the platform's distribution. See the Fee Structure section for full details.

Why No Secondary Trading Until Close?

During both Stage 1 (Escrow) and Stage 2 (Funding):

  • Only the issuer can sell — investors cannot resell yet
  • Solana → ISIN conversions are only available to the issuer (to fill Channel 3 orders)

This ensures orderly primary distribution. Once the offering closes (hard cap met or duration expires), secondary trading and conversions open to all holders.

⚠️ What Happens If Soft Cap Is NOT Reached

If the offering fails to reach soft cap, the security becomes a zero-coupon bond that immediately redeems:

  • Each investor receives back their invested capital (the actual amount they paid)
  • Discounts are respected — if you paid a discounted price, you get back what you paid, not face value
  • Fees still apply (unlike what you might expect):
    • Clearstream fees (if Channel 3 was used)
    • 0.5% brokerage fee on initial security purchase
  • This is structured as a principal distribution, not a "refund"

The security legally existed, was purchased, and is now being redeemed at par value of investment. It's not a cancellation — it's an early maturity.

Key Insight: We're Tokenizing an Existing Agreement

Unlike a typical fundraise where money is collected first and then a deal is made, Sails.to works in reverse: all legal agreements and security pledges must be in place BEFORE tokens are minted. The tokens represent an already-signed agreement. This is why funds can be released as soon as soft cap is reached.

Note: "Project" here can be structured as a traditional loan, zero-coupon instrument, profit-sharing arrangement, or other security types.

3. How Trading Works

Why Not Just Use a Regular DEX?

You might wonder: "Why can't these tokens just trade on Uniswap or Raydium?"

The answer: Because these are real securities with legal restrictions. The law says only eligible investors can own them. An open DEX would let anyone buy them, which breaks securities regulations.

The OTC Interdealer Network Model

"OTC" stands for "Over-The-Counter" — it means trades happen through intermediaries (brokers), not on a public order book anyone can access. The Sails.to OTC network connects all licensed brokers so orders can be matched across the entire network.

Imagine you want to sell a rare painting. You don't just put it on eBay. You work with an art dealer who posts your availability to a network of dealers. A buyer working with a different dealer posts what they're looking for. The network matches them up, and the dealers handle everything. That's how OTC trading works.

Atomic Settlement

All parts of the trade happen in one atomic transaction on Solana:

  • Token transfer from seller's broker to buyer's broker
  • Payment transfer in the opposite direction
  • Fee distribution: ⅔ split among participating brokers (proportionate to their contribution), ⅓ to platform

Either everything succeeds together, or nothing happens. No counterparty risk, no partial fills stuck in limbo.

Multi-Broker Matching

A single buy request can be filled by multiple sellers from different brokers. If you want to buy 10 tokens:

  • Broker A might have 4 available from their client
  • Broker B might have 4 from theirs
  • Broker C contributes the remaining 2

The matching engine aggregates liquidity across the entire network, and all partial fills settle atomically in one transaction.

Key Trading Rules

Rule Why It Exists
Investors never trade directly with each other Brokers ensure both parties are eligible and compliant
Brokers act as "principals" (they buy, then sell) Creates clear accountability and audit trail
All transfers check KYC credentials Tokens can only go to verified, eligible wallets
No secondary trading until offering closes Ensures orderly primary distribution; only issuer sells during distribution period

4. Converting Between Blockchain & Traditional Finance (Channel 3)

During primary distribution, the issuer uses conversions to fulfill Channel 3 (Clearstream) orders. After the offering closes, all token holders can convert freely between forms.

The same investment can exist in two forms:

  1. Solana security token — traded on the blockchain OTC network
  2. ISIN security — Clearstream-registered, traded OTC through traditional finance rails

The Single Source of Truth

The Solana token is the "canonical" (real, original) security. The Clearstream ISIN is a "representation" — essentially a receipt that says "the custodian is holding X tokens on your behalf."

Critical rule: ISIN securities outstanding can never exceed tokens locked in custody. The system is always 1:1 backed.

Selling to a buyer who only uses traditional rails Solana → Clearstream Buyer's bank can only settle via Clearstream

5. The Fee Structure

Sails.to fees are based on whether the investor is known to the issuer, not which channel they use:

Fee Type Amount When Charged Who Pays Split
Brokerage Fee 0.5% All primary purchases (even if soft cap fails) Buyer ⅔ Brokers (proportionate) + ⅓ Platform
Distribution Fee (Unknown Investors) +5.5% When selling to investors NOT already known to issuer Issuer (from proceeds) Brokers + Admin + Platform
Total: 0.5% for known investors, 6% for unknown investors (on successful close)
Clearstream Fees Variable Channel 3 transactions (even if soft cap fails) Investor Clearstream
Secondary Trading Fee 0.5% Every secondary trade on Solana OTC (after offering closes) Buyer ⅔ Brokers (proportionate) + ⅓ Platform
Conversion Fee 0.10-0.25% (capped) When wrapping/unwrapping Solana ↔ Clearstream Requester Platform + Custodian

Fees That Apply Even If Soft Cap Fails

When the offering fails to reach soft cap and redeems as a zero-coupon bond:

  • 0.5% brokerage fee — already deducted at time of purchase
  • Clearstream fees — if Channel 3 was used

The +5.5% distribution fee for unknown investors is NOT charged on failed offerings — that only applies when the offering successfully closes.

The Big Picture

Sails.to creates a bridge between:

  • Traditional finance (regulated securities, professional investors, settlement systems like Clearstream)
  • Blockchain technology (transparent ownership records, programmable compliance, global accessibility)

The smart contracts enforce the rules automatically. The credentials ensure only eligible parties participate. The conversion system lets investments flow between both worlds. And Melusina OS provides all the operational tools to make it actually work in practice.
