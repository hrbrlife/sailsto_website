⛵ Sails.to - How It All Works

What is Sails.to?

Sails.to lets companies raise money by selling digital securities (called "security tokens") to professional investors. You set up your own legal structure, tokenize your offering, and licensed brokers help you distribute it to their investor networks.

Everything settles on Solana — transparent, instant, and automatic. Investors who prefer traditional rails can hold the same security through Clearstream with an ISIN you register yourself.

The key difference from regular crypto: These aren't memecoins. They're real securities with legal restrictions on who can buy them and how they can be traded.

2. Your Legal Structure

When you raise money through Sails.to, you create your own Wyoming DAO Series LLC. This is your legal structure — you own it, you control it, and it holds your securities.

Why a DAO Series LLC?

Self-Custody, Self-Control

Your securities live in your own legal entity. You're not handing assets to a platform or relying on a third party to custody your tokens. The DAO LLC recognizes smart contract governance, so investor rights can be enforced automatically on-chain.

Each DAO LLC contains multiple "Series" — legally separate containers with their own assets and liabilities. This lets you run multiple offerings without them affecting each other.

ISIN Self-Registration

If you want your security available on traditional rails, you can register your own ISIN directly with a National Numbering Agency. You don't need an exchange or a third party — it's just an identifier. Your ISIN Lockbox Series handles the conversion between Solana tokens and ISIN securities.

Think of your DAO LLC like a holding company with separate subsidiaries (Series) for each project. If one project has issues, the others are legally isolated. You control the whole structure, and the blockchain enforces the rules you set.

3. How New Securities Are Created

The Fundraising System

Every offering has two important numbers and two pricing phases:

Term What It Means
Soft Cap The minimum amount that must be raised for the deal to happen
Hard Cap The maximum amount that can be raised
Duration Maximum time the offering stays open

Early Bird (Optional Discount)

From opening until the soft cap is reached, you can offer an optional discount to early investors. This incentivizes people to commit early and helps reach the soft cap faster.

Full Price

Once the soft cap is reached, the discount ends. Additional investments up to the hard cap are at full price ($150,000 per security) until the offering ends.

When Does the Offering End?

The offering closes when either of these happens first:

  • Hard cap is met — maximum funding reached, offering closes immediately
  • Duration expires — the offering period you set passes, offering closes at current level

Once soft cap is reached, the project is live and funds flow to you. Additional investors can still join until the offering ends.

How Channel 3 Works

Some investors only work with traditional securities infrastructure. Channel 3 bridges that gap:

  1. You lock tokens in your ISIN Lockbox Series
  2. Tokens are wrapped 1:1 into ISIN-bearing securities (you self-register the ISIN)
  3. Your paying agent bank distributes them via Clearstream

The same investment, accessible through traditional finance rails, under your control.

Fees

Fees depend on how the investor found your offering — tracked via referral codes. Investors brought in by your own network cost less than those sourced through the broker network. See the Fee Structure section for full details.

Why No Secondary Trading Until Close?

During both Stage 1 (Escrow) and Stage 2 (Funding):

  • Only you can sell — investors cannot resell yet
  • Solana → ISIN conversions are only available to you (to fill Channel 3 orders)

This ensures orderly primary distribution. Once the offering closes (hard cap met or duration expires), secondary trading and conversions open to all holders.

⚠️ What Happens If Soft Cap Is NOT Reached

If your offering fails to reach soft cap, the security becomes a zero-coupon bond that immediately redeems:

  • Each investor receives back their invested capital (the actual amount they paid)
  • Discounts are respected — if they paid a discounted price, they get back what they paid, not face value
  • Fees still apply (unlike what you might expect):
    • Clearstream fees (if Channel 3 was used)
    • 0.5% brokerage fee on initial security purchase
  • This is structured as a principal distribution, not a "refund"

The security legally existed, was purchased, and is now being redeemed at par value of investment. It's not a cancellation — it's an early maturity.

4. How Trading Works

Why Not Just Use a Regular DEX?

You might wonder: "Why can't these tokens just trade on Uniswap or Raydium?"

The answer: Because these are real securities with legal restrictions. The law says only eligible investors can own them. An open DEX would let anyone buy them, which breaks securities regulations.

The OTC Broker Network

"OTC" stands for "Over-The-Counter" — trades happen through licensed brokers, not on a public order book. Each broker has their own investor clients, and brokers trade directly with each other when their clients want to buy or sell.

Imagine you want to sell a rare painting. You don't just put it on eBay. You work with an art dealer who contacts other dealers to find a buyer. A buyer working with a different dealer is looking for exactly that piece. The dealers negotiate, agree on terms, and handle the transaction. That's how OTC trading works.

Atomic Settlement

All parts of the trade happen in one atomic transaction on Solana:

  • Token transfer from seller's broker to buyer's broker
  • Payment transfer in the opposite direction
  • Fee distribution automatically

Either everything succeeds together, or nothing happens. No counterparty risk, no partial fills stuck in limbo.

Multi-Broker Fills

A single buy request can be filled by multiple sellers from different brokers. If someone wants to buy 10 tokens:

  • Broker A might have 4 available from their client
  • Broker B might have 4 from theirs
  • Broker C contributes the remaining 2

Brokers coordinate, and all partial fills settle atomically in one transaction.

Key Trading Rules

Rule Why It Exists
Investors trade through brokers, not directly with each other Brokers ensure both parties are eligible and compliant
All transfers check KYC credentials on-chain Tokens can only go to verified, eligible wallets
No secondary trading until your offering closes Ensures orderly primary distribution; only you sell during distribution period
Settlement is atomic on Solana No counterparty risk — either the whole trade happens or nothing does

You Control the Conversion

The ISIN Lockbox is a Series within your DAO LLC. You (with Trust oversight) control the conversion. When tokens are locked, ISIN securities are issued. When ISIN securities are redeemed, tokens are released. Always 1:1.

No third-party custodian required. You self-custody through your own legal structure.

The Single Source of Truth

The Solana token is the "canonical" (original) security. The ISIN security is a "representation" — essentially a receipt that says "the issuer's lockbox is holding X tokens backing this ISIN."

Critical rule: ISIN securities outstanding can never exceed tokens locked in your lockbox. The system is always 1:1 backed.

6. The Fee Structure

Fees are based on whether the investor is known to you, not which channel they use:

Fee Type Amount When Charged Who Pays Split
Brokerage Fee 0.5% All primary purchases (even if soft cap fails) Buyer ⅔ Brokers (proportionate) + ⅓ Foundation
Distribution Fee (Unknown Investors) +5.5% When selling to investors NOT already known to you You (from proceeds) Brokers + Admin + Foundation
Total: 0.5% for known investors, 6% for unknown investors (on successful close)
Clearstream Fees Variable Channel 3 transactions (even if soft cap fails) Investor Clearstream
Secondary Trading Fee 0.5% Every secondary trade on Solana OTC (after offering closes) Buyer ⅔ Brokers (proportionate) + ⅓ Foundation
Conversion Fee 0.10-0.25% (capped) When wrapping/unwrapping Solana ↔ Clearstream Requester Foundation + Trust

Fees That Apply Even If Soft Cap Fails

When your offering fails to reach soft cap and redeems as a zero-coupon bond:

  • 0.5% brokerage fee — already deducted at time of purchase
  • Clearstream fees — if Channel 3 was used

The +5.5% distribution fee for unknown investors is NOT charged on failed offerings — that only applies when the offering successfully closes.
