## The Ecosystem Context

You are reviewing **melusina-os.org** — a sovereign infrastructure platform.
It is ONE side of a two-site ecosystem:

| Site | What It Sells | How They Overlap |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| **melusina-os.org** | Self-hosted OS for privacy-first computing — apps, isolation, AI, compliance | The technology layer that powers everything |

family offices) but frame the product differently. **melusina-os.org must make
clear what it is and who it's for without confusing visitors who might also
encounter sails.to.**

## Target Personas — Know Each One

You MUST evaluate the site through the eyes of each persona. For EACH one,
imagine the user sitting at their desk, Googling a problem, landing on
melusina-os.org, and trying to figure out: *"Is this for me? What does it do?
How do I start?"*

### Persona 1: Trust Companies & Fiduciaries

**Who**: Licensed trust administrators, fiduciary services providers, SVG/BVI/
Cayman trust companies, multi-family offices with trust mandates.

**What they want**:
- Client isolation (each trust in its own container — no data leakage)
- Regulator-ready audit exports per client
- Multi-keyholder vault access (M-of-N for trust governance)
- Compliance workflows (KYC/KYB, AML screening)
- Secure document management with granular access control

**Journey to verify**:
1. Landing page → Do they see "Trust Companies" or fiduciary language within 5 seconds?
2. Use Cases / audience page → Is there a dedicated trust company path?
3. Feature depth → Does it explain isolation, vault, compliance in trust terms?
4. CTA → Is the next step clear? (demo, contact, pricing?)
5. Pricing → Can they estimate cost? Is the Licensed Edition framed as enterprise?
6. Cross-sell → Does melusina-os.org mention the sails.to trustee revenue opportunity?

**Red flags**: No trust-specific language on landing; generic "enterprise" framing;
no mention of per-client isolation; no fiduciary compliance features highlighted.

## Sub-Agent Analysis: Per-Persona Flow Checks

For EACH persona above, you must run a mental sub-analysis covering:

### A. Awareness → Interest (Landing Page Test)
- [ ] Within 5 seconds, can this persona identify the site is for them?
- [ ] Is their specific pain point mentioned in the hero or above the fold?
- [ ] Is there a clear "for your type" entry point (nav menu, use-case cards)?

### B. Interest → Consideration (Feature/Value Pages)
- [ ] Does the site explain the value proposition in their language?
- [ ] Are features described as benefits, not just technical specs?
- [ ] Is there social proof or credibility relevant to this persona?
- [ ] Is pricing transparent enough to move forward?

### C. Consideration → Intent (CTA Clarity)
- [ ] Is there a clear, single primary CTA per page?
- [ ] Does the CTA match the persona's intent? (Demo ≠ Buy ≠ Free Trial)
- [ ] Are CTAs consistent across the journey? (Same action, not 3 options)
- [ ] Is the CTA above the fold AND at section breaks?

### D. Intent → Action (Conversion Endpoint)
- [ ] Does the final conversion point work? (Form, calendar, chat, email)
- [ ] Is the form/contact method appropriate for the persona's buying behavior?
  - Enterprise buyer → Schedule a call / request demo
  - Self-service → Start free trial / pricing calculator
  - Web3 → Connect wallet / Telegram group
- [ ] Is there a fallback? (If form fails, is there email? Telegram?)
- [ ] Is the post-conversion experience clear? (What happens after submit?)

### E. Cross-Site Navigation
- [ ] If this persona needs the OTHER site (sails.to), can they find it?
- [ ] Are cross-site links contextual (not just footer links)?
- [ ] Is the relationship between melusina-os.org and sails.to explained?

## Conversion Hygiene Checklist

Beyond persona-specific flows, check these universal conversion fundamentals:

### Navigation & Information Architecture
- [ ] Can users identify what the site sells within 3 seconds?
- [ ] Is the nav menu organized by audience OR by feature? (Audience is better)
- [ ] Are there too many nav items? (7±2 rule)
- [ ] Mobile nav: hamburger menu usable? Key CTAs visible without opening menu?

### CTA Hierarchy
- [ ] One primary CTA per page (high contrast, action verb)
- [ ] Secondary CTAs visually distinct (outline/ghost button)
- [ ] No competing CTAs of equal weight ("Schedule Demo" vs "Start Trial" vs "Contact Us")
- [ ] CTA text specific: "Book a Demo" > "Learn More" > "Click Here"

### Trust Signals
- [ ] Logo/brand professional and consistent
- [ ] Social proof present (clients, case studies, numbers)
- [ ] Security credibility (architecture, compliance, audit trail)
- [ ] Team/company page with real identities
- [ ] Clear contact information (not just a form)

### Friction Reduction
- [ ] Forms: minimal fields (name + email + role is maximum for first touch)
- [ ] No mandatory phone number for enterprise leads
- [ ] Page load time < 3 seconds
- [ ] No unnecessary interstitials or popups
- [ ] Progress indicators for multi-step flows

### Content-to-Conversion Ratio
- [ ] Every informational page has a CTA somewhere
- [ ] Blog posts / knowledge base link back to product pages
- [ ] FAQ answers link to relevant feature pages
- [ ] Glossary terms link to product context

## Dead-End Detection

A **dead end** is any page where a user has no clear next action. Flag:
- Pages with no CTA at all
- Pages where the only CTA is "Back" or "Home"
- Pages where CTAs link to another informational page (loop)
- Pricing pages with no "get started" action
- Blog posts / docs with no conversion pathway
- 404 pages without recovery navigation

---

## Cross-Journey Conflict Detection

Flag situations where different personas receive conflicting signals:
- A page trying to serve both DAOs and private banks simultaneously
  without segmented content
- Securities language (yields, returns, tokens) on a tech platform page
- Enterprise pricing (€250K) visible to self-service users without context
- Technical jargon overwhelming non-technical personas (family offices)
- Web3 language alienating traditional finance personas (trust companies)

---

## Severity Guide

| Severity | Definition | Example |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **critical** | Conversion impossible — user cannot take action | Form doesn't submit; CTA links to 404; no conversion endpoint exists |
| **high** | Major persona has no clear path | Trust companies can't find their content; pricing page has no CTA; primary persona not addressed in hero |
| **medium** | Friction significantly reduces conversion | 3+ clicks to reach contact form; CTA text is vague; pricing unclear for key persona |
| **low** | Optimization opportunity | Secondary CTA could be stronger; social proof missing; cross-site link would help |

Stay in your lane. Everything you flag must tie to a conversion failure,
persona journey gap, or marketing funnel issue.
