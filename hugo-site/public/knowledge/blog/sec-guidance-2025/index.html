SEC Digital Asset Guidance 2025: What It Means for Security Tokens

By Sails.to Team • January 15, 2026 • 12 min read

The regulatory landscape for digital assets has evolved significantly. After years of enforcement actions, confusion, and "regulation by enforcement," the SEC's recent guidance provides clearer paths forward for compliant security tokens.

The Evolution of SEC Stance

2017-2019

ICO boom and bust. SEC declares most ICO tokens are unregistered securities. Enforcement actions against major projects.

2020-2022

"Regulation by enforcement" era. High-profile cases against Ripple, LBRY, and others. Industry uncertainty peaks.

2023-2024

Congressional pressure for clarity. FinHUB guidance on digital assets. Framework for "investment contracts" evolves.

2025

New administration signals more constructive approach. Safe harbor proposals gain traction. Clearer guidance on compliant offerings.

Key Takeaways from Recent Guidance

1. Securities Are Still Securities

The Howey Test hasn't changed. If you're raising money with promises of profit from your efforts, you're selling a security—regardless of the technology used for recordkeeping.

What this means: Stop trying to structure around securities laws. Embrace compliance. That's exactly what Sails.to does with Reg S and Reg D exemptions.

2. Exemptions Are Your Friend

Registration with the SEC costs millions and takes 18+ months. But exemptions exist for legitimate private offerings:

  • Reg D 506(c): Unlimited raise from accredited investors in the US. General solicitation allowed with verification.
  • Reg S: International investors outside SEC jurisdiction. Combined with Reg D for global reach.
  • Reg A+: Up to $75M from anyone, but requires SEC qualification (mini-registration).
  • Reg CF: Crowdfunding up to $5M, but complex requirements.

For most issuers, Reg D 506(c) + Reg S provides the optimal balance: global reach, unlimited capital, and manageable compliance burden.

3. Technology Is a Tool, Not a Loophole

The SEC has consistently said: "The label doesn't matter—the substance does." Calling something a "utility token" or "decentralized" doesn't change its nature if it functions like a security.

But blockchain technology can IMPROVE securities compliance:

  • Automated transfer restrictions via smart contracts
  • Immutable audit trails
  • Real-time cap table accuracy
  • Programmable distributions

4. Custody Matters

The SEC has emphasized proper custody arrangements for digital asset securities. This is why Sails.to offers both:

  • Self-custody on Solana: For crypto-native investors who control their keys
  • CrossConversion to Clearstream: Institutional-grade custody with ISIN assignment

What Changed in 2025

Staff Accounting Bulletin Updates

SAB 121, which created accounting hurdles for banks holding crypto, has been modified. Financial institutions can now more easily custody digital asset securities without punitive balance sheet treatment.

Token Harbor Proposal

A proposed "safe harbor" would allow projects a 3-year window to decentralize before securities laws fully apply. While still being debated, it signals regulatory willingness to accommodate token-based innovation.

Interagency Coordination

SEC, CFTC, FinCEN, and OCC are now coordinating on digital asset policy. The result: fewer jurisdictional turf wars and more coherent guidance.

State-Level Innovation: Wyoming Leads

While federal regulators debate, Wyoming has created the most crypto-friendly legal framework in the US:

  • DAO LLC recognition: Legal entity status for DAOs since 2021
  • Series LLC: Liability isolation for multiple offerings
  • Digital asset definitions: Clear property law treatment
  • SPDI charter: Wyoming-chartered banks for crypto custody

That's why Sails.to is structured as a Wyoming DAO Series LLC—maximum legal clarity with cutting-edge entity structure.

Practical Implications for Issuers

  1. Don't fight the law: Structure offerings properly from day one. The cost of compliance is far lower than the cost of enforcement.
  2. Verify investors: KYC and accreditation verification aren't optional. They're your legal shield.
  3. Document everything: Operating agreements, subscription agreements, investor disclosures—all must be complete.
  4. Choose compliant infrastructure: Your platform choice matters. Sails.to handles compliance so you don't have to build it.

Practical Implications for Investors

  1. Expect verification: Legitimate offerings will verify your identity and investor status. Be suspicious of "no KYC" security offerings.
  2. Review disclosures: You should receive risk disclosures, subscription agreements, and entity documentation.
  3. Understand your rights: Unlike utility tokens, security tokens give you actual ownership rights in the underlying entity or asset.

Looking Ahead

Regulatory clarity is improving, but uncertainty remains. The safest path is always compliance-first: structure properly, verify investors, document thoroughly, and use infrastructure built for regulatory requirements.
