        <p>The regulatory landscape for digital assets has evolved significantly. After years of enforcement actions, confusion, and "regulation by enforcement," the SEC's recent guidance provides clearer paths forward for compliant <span class="glossary-term" data-term="security-token">security tokens</span>.</p>
        <h2>The Evolution of SEC Stance</h2>
        <div class="timeline">
            <div class="timeline-item">
                <div class="timeline-date">2017-2019</div>
                <p>ICO boom and bust. SEC declares most ICO tokens are unregistered securities. Enforcement actions against major projects.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="timeline-item">
                <div class="timeline-date">2020-2022</div>
                <p>"Regulation by enforcement" era. High-profile cases against Ripple, LBRY, and others. Industry uncertainty peaks.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="timeline-item">
                <div class="timeline-date">2023-2024</div>
                <p>Congressional pressure for clarity. FinHUB guidance on digital assets. Framework for "investment contracts" evolves.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="timeline-item">
                <div class="timeline-date">2025</div>
                <p>New administration signals more constructive approach. Safe harbor proposals gain traction. Clearer guidance on compliant offerings.</p>
            </div>
        </div>
        <h2>Key Takeaways from Recent Guidance</h2>
        <h3>1. Securities Are Still Securities</h3>
        <p>The Howey Test hasn't changed. If you're raising money with promises of profit from your efforts, you're selling a security—regardless of the technology used for recordkeeping.</p>
        <div class="highlight-box">
            <p><strong>What this means:</strong> Stop trying to structure around securities laws. Embrace compliance. That's exactly what Sails.to does with <span class="glossary-term" data-term="reg-s">Reg S</span> and <span class="glossary-term" data-term="reg-d">Reg D</span> exemptions.</p>
        </div>
        <h3>2. Exemptions Are Your Friend</h3>
        <p>Registration with the SEC costs millions and takes 18+ months. But exemptions exist for legitimate private offerings:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Reg D 506(c):</strong> Unlimited raise from <span class="glossary-term" data-term="accredited-investor">accredited investors</span> in the US. General solicitation allowed with verification.</li>
            <li><strong>Reg S:</strong> International investors outside SEC jurisdiction. Combined with Reg D for global reach.</li>
            <li><strong>Reg A+:</strong> Up to $75M from anyone, but requires SEC qualification (mini-registration).</li>
            <li><strong>Reg CF:</strong> Crowdfunding up to $5M, but complex requirements.</li>
        </ul>
        <p>For most issuers, Reg D 506(c) + Reg S provides the optimal balance: global reach, unlimited capital, and manageable compliance burden.</p>
        <h3>3. Technology Is a Tool, Not a Loophole</h3>
        <p>The SEC has consistently said: "The label doesn't matter—the substance does." Calling something a "utility token" or "decentralized" doesn't change its nature if it functions like a security.</p>
        <p>But blockchain technology can IMPROVE securities compliance:</p>
        <ul>
            <li>Automated transfer restrictions via <span class="glossary-term" data-term="smart-contract">smart contracts</span></li>
            <li>Immutable audit trails</li>
            <li>Real-time <span class="glossary-term" data-term="cap-table">cap table</span> accuracy</li>
            <li>Programmable <span class="glossary-term" data-term="distributions">distributions</span></li>
        </ul>

        </ul>
        <h2>What Changed in 2025</h2>
        <h3>Staff Accounting Bulletin Updates</h3>
        <p>SAB 121, which created accounting hurdles for banks holding crypto, has been modified. Financial institutions can now more easily custody digital asset securities without punitive balance sheet treatment.</p>
        <h3>Token Harbor Proposal</h3>
        <p>A proposed "safe harbor" would allow projects a 3-year window to decentralize before securities laws fully apply. While still being debated, it signals regulatory willingness to accommodate token-based innovation.</p>
        <h3>Interagency Coordination</h3>
        <p>SEC, CFTC, FinCEN, and OCC are now coordinating on digital asset policy. The result: fewer jurisdictional turf wars and more coherent guidance.</p>
        <h2>State-Level Innovation: Wyoming Leads</h2>
        <p>While federal regulators debate, Wyoming has created the most crypto-friendly legal framework in the US:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong><span class="glossary-term" data-term="wyoming-dao-llc">DAO LLC</span> recognition:</strong> Legal entity status for DAOs since 2021</li>
            <li><strong><span class="glossary-term" data-term="series-llc">Series LLC</span>:</strong> Liability isolation for multiple offerings</li>
            <li><strong>Digital asset definitions:</strong> Clear property law treatment</li>
            <li><strong>SPDI charter:</strong> Wyoming-chartered banks for crypto custody</li>
        </ul>
        <p>That's why Sails.to is structured as a Wyoming DAO Series LLC—maximum legal clarity with cutting-edge entity structure.</p>
        <h2>Practical Implications for Issuers</h2>
        <ol>
            <li><strong>Don't fight the law:</strong> Structure offerings properly from day one. The cost of compliance is far lower than the cost of enforcement.</li>
            <li><strong>Verify investors:</strong> <span class="glossary-term" data-term="kyc">KYC</span> and accreditation verification aren't optional. They're your legal shield.</li>
            <li><strong>Document everything:</strong> Operating agreements, subscription agreements, investor disclosures—all must be complete.</li>
            <li><strong>Choose compliant infrastructure:</strong> Your platform choice matters. Sails.to handles compliance so you don't have to build it.</li>
        </ol>
        <h2>Practical Implications for Investors</h2>
        <ol>
            <li><strong>Expect verification:</strong> Legitimate offerings will verify your identity and investor status. Be suspicious of "no KYC" security offerings.</li>
            <li><strong>Review disclosures:</strong> You should receive risk disclosures, subscription agreements, and entity documentation.</li>
            <li><strong>Understand your rights:</strong> Unlike utility tokens, security tokens give you actual ownership rights in the underlying entity or asset.</li>
        </ol>
