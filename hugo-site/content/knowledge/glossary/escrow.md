<section class="term-section">
    <h2>Full Definition</h2>
    <p><strong>Escrow</strong> is a financial arrangement where a third party holds and regulates payment of funds required for two parties in a transaction. The funds are released only when all conditions of the agreement are satisfied. In securities offerings, escrow protects investor funds until specified milestones (like reaching a <a href="/knowledge/glossary/soft-cap/">soft cap</a>) are achieved.</p>
</section>
<section class="term-section">
    <h2>Why It Matters</h2>
    <p>On Sails.to, investor funds are held in escrow during the fundraising period. If the <a href="/knowledge/glossary/soft-cap/">soft cap</a> isn't reached, funds are returned automatically. Once the threshold is met, funds are released to the issuer and securities tokens are distributed to investors.</p>
    <p>This mechanism provides crucial protection: investors know their capital won't be deployed into underfunded ventures, and issuers gain credibility by demonstrating proper fund management practices.</p>
    <p>Sails uses <a href="/knowledge/glossary/smart-contract/">smart contract</a>-based escrow for on-chain transactions, providing transparent, auditable, and automated fund management.</p>
</section>
