<section class="term-section">
    <h2>Full Definition</h2>
    <p><strong>Maturity</strong> (or maturity date) is when a bond's term ends and the issuer must repay the principal (<a href="/knowledge/glossary/nominal-value/">nominal value</a>) to bondholders. Until maturity, bondholders receive periodic <a href="/knowledge/glossary/coupon/">coupon</a> payments. At maturity, they receive the final coupon payment plus the full principal amount.</p>
    <p>Bonds may have maturities ranging from a few months (short-term) to 30+ years (long-term). The maturity period affects both the bond's risk profile and its sensitivity to interest rate changes.</p>
</section>
<section class="term-section">
    <h2>Why It Matters</h2>
    <p>On Sails.to, each bond offering clearly states its maturity date so investors can plan their investment horizon. Longer maturities typically carry more uncertainty and may offer higher yields to compensate.</p>
    <p>For <a href="/knowledge/glossary/crosssecurities/">CrossSecurities</a> bonds, principal repayment at maturity is handled automatically through the platform, with funds distributed to holders regardless of whether they hold on-chain tokens or bankable ISIN securities.</p>
</section>
