        <section id="what-is" class="guide-section">
            <h2>What Is a Wyoming DAO LLC?</h2>
            <p>A <span class="glossary-term" data-term="wyoming-dao-llc">Wyoming DAO LLC</span> is a limited liability company that can be managed algorithmically - through <span class="glossary-term" data-term="smart-contract">smart contracts</span> instead of (or in addition to) human managers.</p>
            <p>In 2021, Wyoming became the first US state to legally recognize Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) as a type of LLC. This means DAOs can:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Own property and assets</li>
                <li>Enter into contracts</li>
                <li>Sue and be sued</li>
                <li>Open bank accounts</li>
                <li>Interact with traditional legal systems</li>
            </ul>
            <p>Without this recognition, DAOs exist in legal limbo - potentially exposing members to unlimited personal liability.</p>
            <div class="highlight-box">
                <p><strong>The key innovation:</strong> Wyoming law explicitly allows smart contracts to serve as the operating agreement and decision-making mechanism for an LLC. Code can be law - legally.</p>
            </div>
        </section>
        <section id="why-wyoming" class="guide-section">
            <h2>Why Wyoming?</h2>
            <p>Wyoming has systematically positioned itself as the most crypto-friendly jurisdiction in the United States:</p>
            <h3>Legislative Leadership</h3>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>2018:</strong> First state to define digital assets as property</li>
                <li><strong>2019:</strong> Created SPDI charter for crypto custody banks</li>
                <li><strong>2021:</strong> DAO LLC recognition statute</li>
                <li><strong>2022-present:</strong> Continued refinement and industry support</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Business-Friendly Environment</h3>
            <ul>
                <li>No state corporate income tax</li>
                <li>No personal income tax</li>
                <li>Strong asset protection laws</li>
                <li>Privacy-respecting registration (no public member disclosure)</li>
                <li>Responsive Secretary of State office</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Legal Clarity</h3>
            <p>While other states are still debating what DAOs are, Wyoming provides clear answers. This certainty reduces legal risk and makes compliance straightforward.</p>
            <div class="info-box">
                <h4>The Delaware Analogy</h4>
                <p>Just as Delaware became the default choice for corporations due to its Chancery Court and developed case law, Wyoming is becoming the default for blockchain entities. Companies like Kraken and Avanti have chosen Wyoming for their banking charters.</p>
            </div>
        </section>
        <section id="series-llc" class="guide-section">
            <h2>The Series LLC Advantage</h2>
            <p>A <span class="glossary-term" data-term="series-llc">Series LLC</span> is a special type of LLC that can create unlimited "series" (sub-LLCs), each with its own assets, liabilities, and members - all under one parent entity.</p>
            <div class="diagram-box">
                <h4>Sails.to Series LLC Structure</h4>
                <div class="structure-visual">
                    <div class="structure-level">Sails.to DAO Series LLC<br><small>(Parent Entity)</small></div>
                    <div class="structure-connector">↓</div>
                    <div class="series-row">
                        <div class="series-box">Series A<br>Real Estate Fund</div>
                        <div class="series-box">Series B<br>Tech Startup</div>
                        <div class="series-box">Series C<br>Renewable Project</div>
                    </div>
                </div>
            </div>
            <h3>Why This Matters</h3>
            <h4>Liability Isolation</h4>
            <p>If Series A has legal issues, Series B and C are protected. Each series' liabilities cannot reach the others - or the parent LLC. This is crucial for a platform hosting multiple offerings.</p>
            <h4>Operational Efficiency</h4>
            <p>Instead of forming a new LLC for every offering (with separate registrations, registered agents, filings), series can be created quickly through the operating agreement.</p>
            <h4>Cost Savings</h4>
            <p>One filing fee for the parent; no separate fees per series. One registered agent. One annual report. Significant savings at scale.</p>
            <h4>Clean Separation</h4>
            <p>Each offering is its own series with:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Its own bank account</li>
                <li>Its own assets and liabilities</li>
                <li>Its own members (token holders)</li>
                <li>Its own governance rules</li>
            </ul>
        </section>
        <section id="how-it-works" class="guide-section">
            <h2>How It Works at Sails.to</h2>
            <h3>The Parent Entity</h3>
            <p>Sails.to DAO Series LLC is the master entity, formed in Wyoming. It:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Provides the legal framework for all offerings</li>
                <li>Maintains the registered agent and state filings</li>
                <li>Establishes the master operating agreement template</li>
                <li>Does NOT hold issuer assets or investor funds</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Series Creation</h3>
            <p>When an issuer launches an offering, we create a new series:</p>
            <ol>
                <li>Series designation registered with parent LLC</li>
                <li>Series-specific operating agreement executed</li>
                <li>Dedicated accounts and wallets created</li>
                <li>Tokens deployed representing membership interests in the series</li>
            </ol>
            <h3>Token = Membership Interest</h3>
            <p>When you purchase tokens, you're acquiring membership interests in a specific series. The <span class="glossary-term" data-term="security-token">security tokens</span> represent:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Pro-rata economic rights (distributions, liquidation proceeds)</li>
                <li>Governance rights as specified in operating agreement</li>
                <li>Information rights (access to financials, reports)</li>
                <li>Transfer rights (subject to compliance restrictions)</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Smart Contract Integration</h3>
            <p>The token smart contract on <span class="glossary-term" data-term="solana">Solana</span> enforces:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Transfer restrictions (<span class="glossary-term" data-term="kyc">KYC</span>/eligibility whitelist)</li>
                <li><span class="glossary-term" data-term="distributions">Distribution</span> automation</li>
                <li>Voting mechanisms</li>
                <li>Supply management</li>
            </ul>
        </section>
        <section id="investor-rights" class="guide-section">
            <h2>Investor Rights & Protections</h2>
            <h3>Limited Liability</h3>
            <p>As a member of a Wyoming LLC, your liability is limited to your investment. You cannot be personally sued for series debts or obligations - just like a corporation shareholder.</p>
            <h3>Economic Rights</h3>
            <p>Specified in each series' operating agreement:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Distribution rights (dividends, income distributions)</li>
                <li>Liquidation priority</li>
                <li>Anti-dilution protections (if applicable)</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Information Rights</h3>
            <p>Members are entitled to:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Annual financial statements</li>
                <li>Access to books and records</li>
                <li>Material updates on offering progress</li>
            </ul>
            <h3>Governance Rights</h3>
            <p>Typically includes voting on:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Major decisions (sale of assets, dissolution)</li>
                <li>Changes to operating agreement</li>
                <li>Selection of managers (in managed LLCs)</li>
            </ul>
            <div class="info-box">
                <h4>Smart Contract ≠ No Legal Rights</h4>
                <p>Your rights exist in the operating agreement - a legally enforceable contract. The smart contract automates execution but doesn't replace your legal protections. If there's a conflict, the operating agreement governs.</p>
            </div>
        </section>
        <section id="governance" class="guide-section">
            <h2>Governance & Voting</h2>
            <h3>Member-Managed vs. Manager-Managed</h3>
            <p>Wyoming law allows two governance models:</p>
            <table class="comparison-table">
                <tr>
                    <th>Aspect</th>
                    <th>Member-Managed</th>
                    <th>Manager-Managed</th>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td>Day-to-day decisions</td>
                    <td>All members vote</td>
                    <td>Manager decides</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td>Major decisions</td>
                    <td>Member vote</td>
                    <td>Member vote</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td>Typical use</td>
                    <td>Small groups, pure DAOs</td>
                    <td>Passive investments, larger groups</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
            <p>Most Sails.to offerings are <strong>manager-managed</strong> - the issuer/sponsor handles operations while investors receive passive economic returns. Major decisions still require member approval.</p>
            <h3>On-Chain Voting</h3>
            <p>For member votes, we use token-weighted on-chain governance:</p>
            <ol>
                <li>Proposal submitted</li>
                <li>Notice period (typically 7 days)</li>
                <li>Voting window opens</li>
                <li>Members vote with their tokens</li>
                <li>Results recorded on blockchain</li>
                <li>Outcome executed (automatically if possible)</li>
            </ol>
            <h3>Quorum & Thresholds</h3>
            <p>Specified in each operating agreement. Common structures:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Ordinary matters:</strong> Majority of votes cast</li>
                <li><strong>Major matters:</strong> 2/3 of outstanding membership interests</li>
                <li><strong>Amendments:</strong> May require higher threshold</li>
            </ul>
        </section>
