## The SUCCES Framework

### Simple
- Can you cut half the words and keep the meaning?
- Is there ONE core message per section?
- Would a smart 16-year-old understand it?
- Securities jargon is acceptable when necessary — but always explain on first use

### Unexpected
- Does the page surprise? Break a pattern?
- "Gap theory" — create curiosity, then satisfy it
- The best sails.to copy creates "wait, you can do THAT?" moments

### Concrete
- Can you picture it? Touch it? Count it?
- Specifics beat abstractions: "0.75% per conversion" > "low fees"
- "~€3-5k for Vienna MTF listing" > "affordable listing"
- Example offerings with real numbers (even illustrative) beat abstract descriptions

### Credible
- Named technology (Solana, Clearstream, ISIN, Cap'n Proto)
- Regulatory references (Reg D, Reg S, Wyoming Series LLC)
- Institutional partnerships and integrations
- Testable claims only — do not overstate product readiness

### Emotional
- Does it connect to feelings? Identity? Consequences?
- Issuer: "Your capital raise shouldn't take 6 months and cost $500K"
- Investor: "Hold it on-chain or cross to bankable ISIN — your choice"
- Broker: "Your own branded OTC desk, live in weeks"

### Stories
- Scenarios that feel recognizable
- "A Mongolian mining company needs $5M…" — the example offering IS a story
- "Your broker calls with an OTC quote…" — walk through the Broker Hop

## The Dinner Test

> "Would you say this sentence to a smart friend at dinner who works in
> finance but doesn't know crypto?"

If it sounds like a whitepaper abstract, rewrite it.
If it sounds like a pitch deck bullet, it's probably fine.

## Kill List — Flag These

### AI-Speak
- leveraging, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art
- next-generation, paradigm, holistic, synergy, ecosystem (when vague)
- "empowering", "revolutionizing", "disrupting"
- "unlock the potential", "harness the power"

### Empty Openers
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape…"
- "As the world of finance continues to change…"
- "In an era of digital transformation…"

### Crypto Clichés
- "trustless" (sails.to is explicitly trust-BASED via credentials)
- "decentralized" when the system is broker-mediated
- "DeFi" — sails.to is explicitly NOT DeFi
- "permissionless" — it's permissioned by design

### Vague Padding
- "comprehensive suite of tools"
- "wide range of features"
- "end-to-end solution" (acceptable sparingly if then immediately made concrete)

### Technical Sins
- Passive voice in CTAs
- Sentences > 30 words
- Paragraphs > 4 sentences
- Headers that don't tell you anything ("Our Approach", "The Solution")

## Sails.to-Specific Editorial Concerns

### Accuracy Matters More Than Catchiness
In securities marketing, a catchy but inaccurate claim is a legal liability.
When editorial quality conflicts with legal precision, legal wins.

### Product Status Accuracy
All products (CrossBonds, CrossShares, CrossRWA) are in **private testing**.
Copy must not claim they are publicly available or launched.

### Audience Sophistication
The primary audience understands finance. Don't over-explain basic concepts
(bonds, equity, KYC). DO explain novel concepts (CrossConversion, soulbound
credentials, Series LLC segregation).

### Melusina OS References
Keep Melusina OS references minimal. Sails.to has its own brand identity:
confident, institutional, confrontational. Reference underlying technology
only where necessary for credibility, not as a co-brand.

## Severities

- **critical**: Factually incorrect financial/legal claim in copy
- **high**: AI-generated sounding paragraph, crypto cliché misrepresenting the product
- **medium**: Wordy section that could be halved, weak headline
- **low**: Minor style inconsistency, opportunity for better phrasing
