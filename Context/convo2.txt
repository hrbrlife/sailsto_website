7. EU Prospectus Regulation (EU 2017/1129)

    “€100,000 minimum denomination = automatic prospectus exemption” – True. Under Article 1(4) of the EU Prospectus Regulation, offers of securities with denominations of at least €100,000 are exempt from the prospectus requirement【European Prospectus Regulation, Art.1(4)(b)】. Similarly, admissions to trading on a regulated market where units are ≥€100k are exempt. This high-denomination exemption has been a longstanding rule to allow wholesale debt issuance without an EU-approved prospectus.

    “Secondary trading price can fall below denomination without triggering prospectus” – True. The prospectus exemption is based on the nominal denomination at issuance, not the price on the secondary market. Even if a €100k-denom bond later trades at, say, €90k, it remains exempt – the crucial factor is the denomination set in the terms. (Recital 18 of the Prospectus Regulation clarifies that subsequent value fluctuations don’t affect the exemption.) Thus, price drops below €100k do not suddenly require a prospectus, as long as the bond’s face value is €100k.

    “Qualified investors only = exemption regardless of denomination” – True. Offers made solely to qualified investors (professional clients) are exempt from the prospectus obligation under Article 1(4)(a)【Prospectus Regulation, Art.1(4)(a)】. Likewise, an offer to fewer than 150 retail investors per member state is exempt. In other words, private placements to qualified investors do not need an EU prospectus, even if the denomination is low. The law provides distinct exemptions: one based on who is targeted (qualified/institutional investors) and one based on a high minimum investment size. (Sources: Regulation (EU) 2017/1129, Art. 1(4) and Art. 3(2))

8. US Reg D and Exchange Act 12(g)

    “Rule 506 has no minimum denomination” – True. Regulation D Rule 506(b or c) places no requirements on security denomination or minimum investment amount. It is an exemption for private offerings to accredited investors (and up to 35 non-accredited in 506(b)), without any dollar floor per security. In practice many Reg D offerings choose high minimum investments for investor suitability, but Reg D itself does not mandate a minimum piece size. (Source: SEC Rule 506, 17 C.F.R. §230.506 – no mention of denominations.)

    “12(g) triggers at >2,000 accredited investors” – True. Under Section 12(g) of the Securities Exchange Act, a private company must register with the SEC if it exceeds 2,000 holders of record (or 500 non-accredited holders) and has $10 million+ in assets【15 U.S.C. §78l(g)(1)】. The JOBS Act in 2012 raised this threshold to 2,000 total holders as long as at most 1,999 are accredited (i.e. you can have up to 2,000 accredited investors without triggering, but if any more – or too many non-accredited – registration is required)【SEC Exchange Act §12(g); JOBS Act §501】. So effectively >2,000 accredited investors would trigger Exchange Act registration. (Source: U.S. Exchange Act §12(g)(1) as amended)

    “Series LLC = each Series counted separately for 12(g)” – Uncertain/Unverified. The treatment of series of a Series LLC for Exchange Act 12(g) registration is not explicitly settled by the SEC in rule or statute. There is no clear-cut SEC guidance or case law on whether each series is considered a separate issuer for the 2,000-holder rule. However, historically the SEC has sometimes treated series of a fund or trust as separate classes for 12(g) counting if interests are segregated. Some practitioners argue that each Series could be viewed as a separate issuer (since liabilities and assets are legally partitioned). No official SEC pronouncement explicitly addresses Series LLCs and §12(g) to date, so this claim cannot be verified from authoritative sources. (Sources: Exchange Act §12(g); no direct SEC guidance on series LLC counting – legal analyses vary.)
